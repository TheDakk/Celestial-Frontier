# P2 — assembly proof (design, 2026-09-20; runs after the four crabs are rigged)

Goal: prove that painted parts interchange within a template, which is what makes procedural variety combinatorial.
Subjects: two accepted painted crabs of the same template (brachyuran), e.g. Crab (host) and Mud Crab (donor).
Steps: (1) take the donor's near claw parts (palm, fixed finger, dactyl) from its admitted atlas; (2) fit them onto the
host's rig at the host's claw landmarks by the template's rest proportions (uniform scale by the ratio of claw base →
palm lengths, rotation to the host's claw axis), producing a new atlas and binding for the host with the donor claw;
(3) run the shared observed split and the static rows exactly as for any fit; (4) native rows and a still beside the
two originals. Gates: exact rest, planted drift, seams (the join at the claw base is the one new seam; `maxGapPx`
reports it), limits, and Nick's eye. Both-way control: a donor part from a different template must be refused by
the template check before any fit. Nothing here changes records, masters or the hidden contract.
