# 2 · What this release broke or missed

## 2.1 CF1806-01 · The `size` clamp and the `size` wrap disagree, and the clamp permanently rewrites honestly-bred creatures · **high — remove one line**

This release ships **two different fixes for the same problem**, in the same release, and they contradict each other.

```js
// battleStats — WRAPS                                              (17895, new)
const sz = ((+g.size||0) % FA_SIZE.length + FA_SIZE.length) % FA_SIZE.length;

// _sanitizeSavedGenome — CLAMPS                                    (16596, new)
if('size' in g) g.size = cl(g.size, 0, FA_SIZE.length-1);
```

**Honest saves carry `size > 5`.** `crossGenome`'s mutation list includes `size` and never wraps it, and `evolveGenome` runs on every breed and mutates it again. Running the build's own `makeGenome` and `crossGenome` over 500 lineages:

| generation | lineages with `size > 5` | max seen |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | 52 / 500 — **10.4%** | 9 |
| 10 | 50 / 500 — **10.0%** | 12 |
| 20 | 56 / 500 — **11.2%** | 14 |

**What one reload does to those creatures.** In session, v1.8.6 behaves exactly as promised — the card and the fight both read `size % 6`. Then `_sanitizeSavedGenome` runs on the player's own codex on every load, `_storeSpecies` keeps the genome by reference, and the save writer writes the clamped value back. The rewrite is permanent after one load:

| stored `size` | in session | after one reload (clamped to 5) |
|---|---|---|
| 6 | **"tiny"**, vit +0, agi −0 | **"titanic"**, vit +20, agi −10 |
| 7 | "small", vit +4 | "titanic", vit +20 |
| 9 | "large", vit +12 | "titanic", vit +20 |
| 12 | **"tiny"**, vit +0 | **"titanic"**, vit +20 |

Portrait scale, voice pitch (`sizeF` reads `g.size % 6`), body-length dial and the "Size Classes" collection slot all move with it. A share code exported before the reload no longer matches one exported after.

**And the clamp buys nothing.** Its own comment justifies it with *"a hand-edited `size:1e6` bought +4,000,000 vitality, and `encodeCreature` carried it into another player's duel box intact."* The wrap in the same release already closes that: `1e6 % 6 = 4` → **+16 vitality**, against a legitimate maximum of +20. `normGenome` leaves `size` as `Math.abs((+o.size)|0)`, so the share-code path is closed by the wrap too.

**Fix: delete line 16596**, or make it the same wrap as 17895. The wrap alone is correct, sufficient, and does not touch honest data.

**This one is partly on me.** Round 8's CF1805-06 named two remedies for one problem — clamp the save, and close the display/mechanics divergence — and shipping both created the conflict. The divergence was the real bug; the clamp was belt-and-braces that turned out to be the harmful half.

Worth noting for symmetry: `color`, `form`, `body`, `loco`, `trait`, `diet`, `head`, `skin`, `tail`, `pattern`, `eyes`, `behavior` and `habitat` all drift the same way and are all read `% ARRAY.length` everywhere. `size` is now the only gene whose load path clamps while every consumer wraps.

## 2.2 CF1806-02 · The training positioning fix omits the dock allowance its sibling makes explicit · **P1 · phones**

The new rule reserves **24 px** at the bottom of the viewport. The rule two lines above it, for the one board that already had this contract, reserves **126 px plus the safe-area inset** — and says why:

```css
/* Nick 2026-07-28: steps 5 and 7 point at dock chips — the card ends above them */
body.training #panel{max-height:min(var(--tut-cap,60dvh),
  calc(100dvh - var(--tut-bot,150px) - 126px - env(safe-area-inset-bottom,0px))) !important}   /* 1795 */

body.training #log.tutpri, body.training #codex.tutpri,
body.training #chpanel.tutpri, body.training #records.tutpri{
  top:calc(var(--tut-bot,150px) + 8px) !important;
  bottom:auto !important;
  min-height:0 !important;
  max-height:var(--tut-cap,calc(100dvh - var(--tut-bot,150px) - 24px)) !important}            /* 1876 */
```

The mobile board contract pins those four at `bottom: calc(142px + env(safe-area-inset-bottom,0px))` precisely to clear the dock. The new rule releases `bottom` and does not put the allowance back.

**Measured**, with `body.training` on and `#chpanel` raised — the state training step 20 (`charter-first`, `allow:['#chbtn','#chpanel']`) creates:

| device | viewport | dock reachability |
|---|---|---|
| **iPhone SE** | 667 | **0%** — all six controls, 63/63 blocked by `#chpanel` |
| **Galaxy S8** | 740 | **0%** — all six |
| iPhone 14 Pro | 852 | **19%** dock row 1 · 94% row 2 |
| iPad mini | 1133 | 95% — clear |

`#codexbtn`, `#logbtn`, `#cargobtn`, `#chbtn`, `#setbtn` and `#helpbtn` all measure zero on the two shortest phones.

**Honesty note on the method:** I forced that state directly (`body.training` + `.tutpri` + a representative `--tut-bot`) rather than walking training to step 20, because my harness still walls at step 7 on phones. I checked the real published value — `--tut-bot = cr.bottom + 10` ≈ 298 on an iPhone SE, `--tut-cap = max(140, H − cr.bottom − 24)` = 355 — and the board still runs to 6 px from the viewport bottom, so the real geometry is the same or worse. The CSS omission is certain; the live step-20 walk is not something I could complete.

The three steps that raise a board are 5 (`#log`), 8 (`#codex`) and 20 (`#chpanel`), so this is on the normal path.

**Fix:** give the new rule the same `min(...)` with the dock and safe-area terms the `#panel` rule already has.

**And a note on the gate that missed it:** the release notes describe 80 new layout checks that sample *the training card* across ten viewports. That is the right check for CF1805-01 and it is why CF1805-01 is genuinely fixed. It is also why this is not caught — the card is fine; it is everything *below* the raised board that is not. The assertion wants to be "at every training step, every control the player can legitimately press is the topmost element at its own coordinates," not just the card.

## 2.3 CF1806-03 · The charter rate-limit costs a reload, not ten minutes · **medium**

```js
let _chRollMono = -1e9;                                    // 24638 — module-level, in memory
...
if(_chRollMono > -1e8 && (_rm - _chRollMono) < 600) return; // 24653
```

The units are right (`perfTime()` is seconds, so 600 = ten minutes) and the intent is right. But `_chRollMono` is a plain module `let`, reset on every page load, and the first roll of each load always passes. `chWeek` is loaded verbatim with no repair (`chWeek=num(data.chw,-1)`). So the cost per slate is one reload, measured in seconds — not ten minutes.

**This is the same shape as CF1805-05, which is still open** (`doHarvest` and `_hvMono` are byte-identical to v1.8.5, comment included). Two exploit mitigations, one round apart, both built on an in-memory monotonic stamp:

> **A monotonic in-memory stamp is the right instinct and the wrong storage.** The clock you are defending against is the device clock, and whoever can wind that can also press F5. A per-session limit cannot bind an attacker who controls the session.

**What actually bites is a load-time bound, and you have already written it once.** `_hvFloor = Math.max(0, _atL − HARVEST_CD)` clamps `conq.t` using `_atL`, the **save's own** stamp — persisted data, not the boot clock — and that is why the harvest exploit yields one cycle per reload rather than unlimited. `_atL` is already in scope at the `chWeek` load line. CF1720-06's objection ("boot is when `Date.now()` is least trustworthy") does not apply to an `_atL`-derived bound, because `_atL` is not the boot clock.

## 2.4 CF1806-04 · The chip repaint does not cover the case CF1802-03 exists for · **low**

The repaint works when an objective exists. When it does not — the stall chip standing in for a missing goal, `_nbEarly = (!g && _stall>=10) ? _nextBest() : null` — the repaint lands in `_chBadge`'s early-out with `_stall` now 0, and the chip **vanishes** where it previously stayed. It returns after ten more pointer events, still as a suggestion, still with a truthy `dataset.go`, so tapping it still cannot open the log.

That is the exact population CF1802-03 identified as *"the player most likely to quit."*

# 2.7 · The audio arc, closed — and this is the good news of the round

CF1805-03 was the last piece of it. Re-running the source-truth voice model — `voiceOf`, `_VOICE`, `_TEMPER_BOLD`, `_blendVoice`, `hashInt`, `makeGenome` extracted verbatim from v1.8.6 — over 200,000 procedural fauna:

| | v1.8.2 | **v1.8.6** |
|---|---|---|
| distinct voices | 533 of 20,000 — a closed set of 18 × 6 × 5 = **540** | **199,707 of 200,000** |
| creatures sharing a voice with another | **97.3%** | **0.15%** |
| chance a 50-creature collection holds a duplicate | **91.3%** | **0.6%** |
| chance a 100-creature collection holds one | ~100% | 2.5% |
| voices pinned at the 6 kHz clamp ceiling | 1.98% | **0.83%** |
| voices pinned at the 60 Hz floor | 0.93% | 0.64% |

CF1802-20 folded four more genes in and CF1805-03 made all five of them index correctly. Between them the voice went from a 540-entry vocabulary that a player exhausted at about fifty creatures to effectively unique-per-creature. The `bat` `f0` drop from 5200 to 3600 plus `_hiTaper` roughly halved the ceiling saturation.

**That closes the audio arc.** Determinism holds, the toggles gate and persist, pitch tracks the displayed size tier, damage scaling is real, zero assets ship, the ambience bed stops on sound-off, and the vocabulary is now wide enough that a listening test would produce a real answer. That was the precondition I put on §15 two rounds ago, and it is met.

*A retraction, caught in the writing:* my first run of this reported **1 distinct voice out of 200,000** — every creature identical — which would have been a catastrophic regression. It was my extractor: `_TEMPER_BOLD` is new since I built the model, so `voiceOf` threw on every call and returned `_VOICE.legacy` from its own catch. Fourth false positive I have caught in two rounds, and the reason I check every headline number against the thing it claims to measure before it ships.

# 3 · Checked and clean

Listing these because they cost time and the negative results are worth having.

- **`voiceOf`'s five new moduli** — `FA_TRAIT`/`FA_BODY`/`FA_LOCO`/`FA_DIET`/`FA_SENSE` are 25/16/18/6/10, all > 1 so `_vw`'s `/(n−1)` cannot divide by zero, and all destructured to outer-IIFE scope well before `voiceOf`. Drifted genes are handled by the `% n`.
- **Duel XP regression shape** — cannot recur; see §1.
- **The chip repaint's mechanics** — `_chBadge` finds the existing `#chchip` and skips the `if(!el)` block, so the listener is never re-bound; `go` is read before the timer is scheduled; `_chBadge → renderQuestLog` does not recurse. The "repaint closes the log the click just opened" race needs `g` to go null inside one macrotask, which nothing does.
- **CSS specificity for the new rule** — `(1,2,1)` with `!important` correctly beats `#log{top:auto !important}` and the desktop `#records{top:auto !important;bottom:60px}`. Scoped to `body.training`, so inert outside training, and `_tutPri` clears `.tutpri` when the tutorial ends. `--tut-bot`/`--tut-cap` are published on `document.documentElement`, so they resolve on all four ids. `#vistabox` is correctly excluded — it has its own `.vcard` contract.
- **`size` overflow paths** — `JSON.parse("1e999")` → `Infinity`, but `cl()` rejects non-finite and `normGenome`'s `Number.isFinite` guard does the same, so `Infinity % 6 → NaN` never reaches `battleStats`.
- **`brood` and `fed` clamps** in `breedPair`/`feedPair` — the only consumers already clamp, and the card renders `delta === 0` as "no change" rather than "+0". Negative deltas still work.
- **`trueOdds`' new key** — `runDuel` reads only the seeds and the stat blocks, all of which the key covers; two states that hash alike do fight alike. `_oddsMemo` keeps its 400-entry clear.
- **Build integrity** — one `<script>`, `node --check` passes, both `<style>` blocks brace-balanced.

# 4 · The pattern, round 9

Rounds 2–5: fixes that could not run. Round 6: fixes with the wrong value. Round 7: fixes that ran everywhere instead of only where needed. Round 8: fixes that diagnosed the problem in a comment and then did not act on it.

**Round 9's is new, and it is a better problem to have: two correct fixes for one bug, shipped together, that disagree.**

CF1806-01 is the pure case. The wrap is right. The clamp is right *in isolation*. Together they mean the game computes one thing in memory and stores another, and the discrepancy is applied silently to ten percent of bred creatures on their next load. Neither author of either line did anything wrong; nobody asked what the other line does.

CF1806-02 is the same shape at one remove: a correct new rule that does not inherit a constraint its sibling encodes two lines above it.

**The remedy is the same one, sharpened:** when a fix touches a value, find every other place that value is read or written, and make them agree. For `size` that is `battleStats`, `describeSpecies`, `speciesPortrait`, `voiceOf`, the collection slots, `normGenome` and `_sanitizeSavedGenome` — seven readers, one of which now disagrees with the other six. A grep for the field name before the fix ships would have caught it, and it is cheaper than the fix.

The trajectory is genuinely good, and worth saying plainly: eight of the nine round-8 items are resolved or correctly mitigated, two of the fixes are better than what I proposed, and one of them pre-empted a failure mode I would have missed. The list is getting shorter and the reasoning behind each fix is getting better.
