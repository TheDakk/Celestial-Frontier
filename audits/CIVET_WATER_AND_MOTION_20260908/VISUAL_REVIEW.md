# Visual review — water and articulated Civet

Reviewed actual final desktop environment canvas and phone native Environment screenshot, plus
native motion stills from the first run (motion source unchanged through the filter-state fix).
The independent source reviewer lists exact still files and findings in SOURCE_REVIEW.md.
This is agent visual review, not Nick’s acceptance or a physical-device session.

The five unfinished residents are gone from the study. The four paws meet shallow water through
small foreground waterlines, low contrast submerged shadows and broken ripples. The original
landscape remains intact and the Civet has retained its accepted anchor. At phone scene scale,
the animal and water treatment are necessarily small; the Creature tab is the motion review.

Two chest rises are clearly stronger than the earlier near-invisible breath. Anticipation lowers
and retracts the head before the forward/up thrust; the tail changes independently. No obvious
fold or detached body piece appears in the reviewed poses. The four planted legs flex subtly,
still showing some image deformation. One projected texture cannot expose convincing hidden
limb surfaces, true footfalls or a turn. Separately painted overlapping parts or a proper 3D rig
are needed for that next level; this batch does not claim to have delivered it.

The supplied Earth sheet supports the natural anatomy, restrained color and directional painted
fur. The Civet still reads a little warmer and sharper than the diffuse wet landscape. Fine fur
fringe/whisker remnants, more unified material softness and final human art acceptance remain
open. Stronger filters alone cannot finish it. Alien plants continue to follow their own supplied
organic fans/pods/fronds/porous growth direction without overwriting seeded forms or biome mapping.

The native pixel/motion controls support visible change, contact, fixed paws and finite lifetime;
they do not measure beauty or fluidity. Six native canvas recordings remain in the final output
for review. No new artwork generation, native game integration or unfinished roster deletion.
