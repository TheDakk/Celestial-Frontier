# C213 bird legs and insect mouthparts

## Implemented

A packet-local `reviewed-presence.json` can now carry an exact master/subject/prompt-bound adult-Mayfly review through G1 and into a new intake. Intake independently rechecks the declaration and retains its input hashes. Only the functional chewing `mandible` actuator is removed. All leg, antenna, wing, coverage, ground, family and static rules remain. The two reviewed originals are listed in phase3-review-index.json; they are not gallery admissions.

Biological evidence: adult Mayflies have nonfunctional mouthparts, unlike their aquatic naiads ([University of Kentucky Entomology](https://entomology.mgcafe.uky.edu/node/1619)); [NC State Entomology](https://genent.cals.ncsu.edu/insect-identification/order-ephemeroptera/) describes the adult mouthparts as vestigial. This is not a rule for the entire soft-mouth profile. In particular it never excuses Mosquito, Scorpionfly, Bee, beetle, damselfly or dobsonfly jaws on a name/profile alone.

The full source inspection found raised veined wing surfaces and no separate projecting chewing jaw on both reviewed Mayflies. Observed wing-surface counts are explicitly not declarations that the complete biological wing inventory is visible. The review does not approve their antenna proportions or other painted anatomy.

## Held with explicit policy boundary

Bird far-thigh/far-shin failures are not proof that the leg is hidden. The C203 Egret original visibly has two complete exposed lower legs and two ground contacts; its 25% mapped far-thigh coverage is a placement error. Assigning it an absent/hidden leg would excuse the wrong problem. A fully hidden far leg also cannot borrow the near leg's ground support: current hidden-chain inference is specifically the final crab pair, not an arbitrary bird chain. No hidden bird declaration or invented foot is enabled.

A future bird occlusion declaration must bind the exact source; identify the actual occluder polygon and visible distal leg/foot if present; preserve both biological chains and independent contact requirements; and reject an erased near leg, erased far foot, duplicate foot, one-contact source, stale declaration and unsupported occluder. A low coverage score or broad body silhouette alone is not that witness. These unresolved bird cases remain refused rather than receiving an unproved blanket exemption.

Antennae remain required. Chewing insects need a better anatomically complete reference, addressed in phase6. Phase3 does not treat all insect mouthparts as absent.
