## Critical

### CF1715-01 — "Restart training" destroys the player's entire expedition, behind a tooltip that promises it won't

**Reproduced in a live browser.** This is the most serious defect in the build.

The control at line 2038 reads:

```html
<span id="retrainopt" class="fsopt" role="button" tabindex="0"
  data-tip="Run the 21-lesson Field Training again — your expedition, creatures and progress are untouched.">Restart</span>
```

It is not untouched. `startTutorial()` wipes the Compendium on entry (24481):

```js
/* a resumed half-finished training leaves only practice debris — clear it */
for(const id of [...codex.keys()]) removeFromCodex(id);
_tutSnap={stats:{...stats}, pstats:{...pstats}, ach:[...unlocked], essence:essence};
_tutPreIds=new Set(codex.keys());
```

Two problems in three lines. The wipe runs **before** `_tutPreIds` is captured, so `_tutPreIds`
is empty and `_tutCleanup`'s "remove only what training added" loop (24387) removes everything.
And `_tutSnap` preserves `stats`, `pstats`, `unlocked` and `essence` — **not** `codex`, `cargo`,
`cgx`, `items`, `equip` or `equipAff`. Then on the way out, 24401:

```js
try{ cargo.clear(); cgx.clear(); items.clear();
     for(const k in equip) delete equip[k]; for(const k in equipAff) delete equipAff[k]; _eqOpen=null;
```

whose comment reads *"A fresh recruit owns nothing else, so a clean sweep is safe."* That
assumption holds for a recruit and fails for everyone else.

Measured, driving the real ⚙ Settings → Gameplay → Restart control on a save with three
specimens, three materials, three item stacks and three equipped pieces:

| | codex | cargo | cgx | items | equipped |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| before | 3 | 3 | 1 | 3 | 3 |
| after the Restart tap | **0** | 3 | 1 | 3 | 3 |
| after leaving training | **0** | **0** | **0** | **0** | **0** |

Essence (4,321) and stats survive; everything the player collected, mined, crafted and equipped
does not. The confirmation is a single re-tap that reads `Restart? — confirm` — it warns about
nothing, because the developer believes there is nothing to warn about.

**One mitigation exists and is worth surfacing:** `cfcc_save_v2_bak` still holds the pre-wipe
state (it is written at load time, 16415), so a player who has not yet reloaded can recover by
copying it over `cfcc_save_v2`. On the next page load `loadSave()` succeeds on the wiped save and
overwrites the backup — after that it is gone.

**Fix:** snapshot and restore `codex`, `cargo`, `cgx`, `items`, `equip`, `equipAff` in
`_tutSnap`/`_tutCleanup`; capture `_tutPreIds` **before** the codex wipe; and gate the whole
control behind an explicit destructive confirm when `tutDone && (codex.size || items.size || cargo.size)`.

### CF1715-02 — After restarting, training dead-ends permanently at step 4 of 21

**Reproduced in a live browser**, on the exact journey a real player takes: fresh game → skip
training → ⚙ Settings → Restart → play forward.

`_tutEnsureEarth()` runs at the end of *every* training, however it ended (24409–24425), and
writes Earth into the Star Atlas:

```js
if(!logMap.has('p133')){ … logMap.set('p133', {id:'p133', title:'Earth', …}); }
homeId='p133';
```

`startTutorial()` never clears `logMap`. So on any restart, Earth is already charted — and step 4
asks the player to chart it. The event the step waits on cannot fire, because `addToLog` refuses
duplicates (14099):

```js
function addToLog(d){
  if(!d.logId || logMap.has(d.logId)) return;
```

The card's button has flipped to `data-act="del"` / "✓ In Star Atlas" (13793), which the step's
own whitelist blocks (13949: `if(!(s0 && s0.acts && s0.acts.includes(a0))) return;` with
`acts:['add']`). The highlight selector `#panel [data-act="add"]` matches nothing, so
`_tutSpot` hides itself and there is **no visual cue at all**. And `allow:['#panel']` prevents
opening the Atlas to delete the entry.

Measured at step 4:

```
step:                4 / 21
body:                "Chart it. Tap + Add to Star Atlas on Earth's card…"
spot visible:        false          ← no highlight
spot selector match: 0 elements
card button:         "✓ IN STAR ATLAS"  data-act="del"
after 5 presses:     still 4 / 21
```

The only exits are **Skip training** — which triggers the CF1715-01 cargo/item/equipment wipe —
or a full Reset. Three reachable entry paths, not one: the Settings restart; quitting and
reloading mid-training after step 4 (boot restarts training at 25384); and quitting on the finale
card, because `_tutCleanup` runs in `finale.enter` before the button is pressed.

**Fix:** in `startTutorial`, `logMap.delete('p133'); if(homeId==='p133') homeId=null;` — or give
step 4 an `enter` guard that auto-advances when `logMap.has('p133')`.

**Chained together, CF1715-01 and CF1715-02 mean one tap on a control labelled "your progress is
untouched" destroys the Compendium, strands the player in an unwinnable tutorial, and the only
way out destroys everything else.** These two should ship together.

### CF1715-03 — Burn ticks 26 times per duel while regen is halved; the strongest ability in the game bypasses defence entirely

Both live in the same block, four lines apart (17588 vs 17593):

```js
if(hpA>0 && A.ab.regen){ _rA=Math.round(maxA*A.ab.regen*0.5); hpA=Math.min(maxA, hpA+_rA); }
…
if(A.ab.burn && hpB>0){ _bB=Math.max(1,Math.round(maxB*A.ab.burn)); hpB=Math.max(0, hpB-_bB); }
```

The loop is `for(let round=0; round<26 …)` (17580) and advances **once per half-turn**, so each
fighter strikes 13 times but the block runs 26. `regen` carries a compensating `*0.5`. `burn`
does not. Cinderburn (`burn:0.05`, 16816) therefore deals `26 × 5% = 130% of the target's maximum
HP` over a full duel, ignoring resilience, dodge, `taken` and `cap` completely — it can kill an
untouched opponent without a single strike landing.

Measured with the game's own `runDuel`, 4,000 seeded duels, identical stats both sides:

| matchup | win rate |
|---|---|
| Cinderburn vs identical no-ability | **98.3%** |
| Mend V (`regen:0.054`, comparable magnitude) vs identical no-ability | 89.8% |
| Cinderburn with the same `*0.5` regen already has | 86.2% |

Same root cause, lower severity: `rampA+=A.ab.ramp` (17591) also accrues 26 times against 13
strikes, so Wildfire (`ramp:0.06`) reaches 2.5× ferocity.

**This also undercuts the v1.7.13 balance claim.** "Every archetype now wins between 42% and X%"
is true of the 17 entries in `ARCHETYPES` — Smite and Roulette are genuinely fixed, at 51.9% and
47.6%. But the pass never touched the 55 hand-written entries in `ABILITY_THEMES` (16812–16879),
and players roll from a pool of both. Cinderburn is now the strongest ability in the game by a
wide margin.

**Fix:** apply `*0.5` to both burn lines (17593–17594), matching the regen treatment — or move
burn/regen/ramp out of the half-turn cadence entirely.

### CF1715-04 — Every exact-HP victory tie is awarded to the player; a perfect mirror match is an 80% player win

Line 17598:

```js
const winner = (hpA<=0&&hpB<=0)?null : hpA<=0?'B' : hpB<=0?'A' : (hpA/maxA>=hpB/maxB?'A':'B');
```

This is the same `>=` bias that CF-CR-007 already fixed for *initiative* twelve lines earlier —
the comment at 17530 even says so: *"`>=` handed side A EVERY agility tie — 93.5% first-slot win
rate across 5,000 exact mirrors."* The victory condition kept it. Side A is always the player:
all three call sites are `runDuel(mine, theirs)` (17870 friendly duel, 19891 conquest, 24377
training).

Measured, 4,000 seeded duels with **identical stat blocks on both sides**:

| matchup | reached the 26-round cap | ended in an exact HP-fraction tie | player won |
|---|---|---|---|
| bulwark tank mirror | 100% | 58.6% | **79.9%** |
| low-ferocity mirror | 100% | 64.9% | **82.8%** |

A defensive build the player cannot out-damage but can out-survive is a free conquest win.

**Fix:** break the final tie with the cascaded seeded coin already written at 17533–17534, and
reserve `null` for genuine parity.

### CF1715-05 — 59 of 546 class ability slots reference archetypes that do not exist; 26 classes have no innate ability at all until level 3

`ARCHETYPES` (16895–16913) defines the ids `shock` and `roulette`. `CLASSES` (16922–17015) names
those two by their *hook* names instead:

```js
['Brawler','M',0,'stun','smite','enrage'],       // 16930
['Chaos Mage','C',3,'gambit','enrage','dot'],    // 17005
```

and the lookup silently discards them (17048):

```js
const ar=ARCHETYPES.find(a=>a.id===cls.verbs[i]); if(!ar) continue;
```

Parsing both tables directly: **59 dead slots out of 546 (10.8%)**, and **26 of 182 classes have
a dead *first* verb**, so they grant nothing at levels 1–2. Affected classes include Brawler,
Ronin, Sorcerer, Cryomancer, Chronomancer, Psion, Shapeshifter, Bard, Spellbreaker, Frost Knight,
Cursed One, Mutant, and — at tier 9 — Eldritch Horror. A Brawler is described as one that
"staggers the foe clean out of its next strike" and never staggers anything.

This also means the design premise stated at 16915 — that innate class arts are more reliable
than rolled abilities — is false for a seventh of the roster, and that the v1.7.13 balance
measurements were taken against a field where 11% of innate slots were silently empty.

**Fix:** rename the two verbs in `CLASSES` to `'shock'` and `'roulette'`, and add a boot-time
assertion that every class verb resolves.

### CF1715-06 — The defensive half of the stat sheet is unplayable

`dmg = att.fer*(1+ramp)*(0.8+r()*0.5) - def.res*0.45` (17555) subtracts mitigation **before**
every multiplier, and the floor is a flat `Math.max(2, …)` (17565). Thirteen strikes × 2 = 26
total damage, against HP pools of `vit*3`. So a low-ferocity build's output collapses to the
floor while an offensive build's scales without limit.

Measured, 4,000 seeded duels, **identical total power (196) on both sides**:

| | wins |
|---|---|
| `vit 90 / fer 10 / res 80 / agi 8 / ins 8` | **0.0%** — 0 of 4,000 |
| `vit 20 / fer 80 / res 20 / agi 40 / ins 36` | 100% |

Not "rarely" — never. This matters beyond player builds: flora carry stat weights
`[1.3, 0.6, 1.45, 0.4, 1.25]` (17421), i.e. high vitality and resilience, low ferocity, so the
kingdom is mechanically incapable of winning a duel.

**Fix:** scale the damage floor with the attacker's ferocity, e.g.
`Math.max(Math.round(att.fer*0.10), Math.round(dmg))`, so mitigation caps near 90% rather than 100%.

### CF1715-07 — `minePending` has no lower bound; a backward clock correction refunds the finite mining reserve

```js
function minePending(ps){
  if(!autoExtractorOwned() || !mined.has(ps)) return 0;
  return Math.min(30, Math.floor((Date.now()-(mined.get(ps)||0))/6e5));   // 20216 — capped above, not below
}
```
```js
let loads=1+minePending(ps);
loads=Math.min(loads, R-n0);
mined.set(ps, Date.now());            // 20228 — the live clock, stored unvalidated
…
mineX.set(ps, n0+loads);              // 20268
stats.mines=(stats.mines||0)+loads;   // 20273
```

If `mined.get(ps)` is in the future, `minePending` returns a negative number, `loads` goes
negative, the ore loop never runs — and `mineX.set(ps, n0+loads)` **subtracts** from the world's
depletion counter. `stats.mines` decrements too.

The save loader clamps *saved* stamps carefully (16213). The *live* write at 20228 has no such
guard, and `craftItem`'s auto-extractor restamp (22067) bulk-writes to every mined world at once.
As an exploit this is a clock-set-forward-then-back cycle that yields ~30 loads of ore per pull of
reserve, and can drive `mineX` negative so `R-n0 > R` and the reserve is effectively unlimited.
More importantly it **fires accidentally on any backward NTP or DST correction**, silently
corrupting depletion state and the mined counter.

**Fix:** `let loads=Math.max(1, 1+minePending(ps));` and clamp the stamp on write against a
persisted high-water mark.

### CF1715-08 — Nine primary controls are under the touch minimum on every device between 901 and 1599px

This is the systemic gap flagged last round as CF173-02, and it is still open. It is no longer
that the band has *no* rules — the desktop block at 1852 lays out a perfectly good command line —
it is that **every device-hardening fix in the file terminates at `max-width:900px`**: the 44px
touch sweep (1766), the safe-area insets (1492, 1748–1780), the 16px input floor that stops iOS
focus-zoom (1898), and the toast collision lane (1892).

Measured across the 21-device sweep. Nine controls fall under 44px on **exactly and only** the
three touch devices in the band:

| control | size in the band | devices |
|---|---|---|
| `#bell` `#setbtn` `#helpbtn` | **34 × 34** | Surface Pro 912 · iPad Pro 13 1024 · iPad landscape 1180 |
| `#recbtn` | **36 × 36** | same three |
| `#logbtn` `#codexbtn` `#cargobtn` `#chbtn` `#pcdxbtn` | **32px tall** | same three |

Sub-44px interactive elements per device, whole sweep:

```
phones + small tablets (≤900px, touch)   134 – 139
Surface Pro / iPad Pro / iPad landscape  231 – 258     ← the band
laptops + desktops (≥1280px, mouse)       31 –  37
```

The three band devices carry roughly **1.8× the touch-target debt of a phone** — they are the only
touch devices in the fleet that receive neither the phone hardening nor the mouse exemption. They
also lead the sweep on elements sitting under the safe-area inset (61–65 vs 49–50 on phones).

**Fix:** change the gate on the touch-target, safe-area and input-size rules from
`@media (max-width:900px)` to `@media (pointer:coarse), (max-width:900px)`. That one edit closes
most of this band.

### CF1715-09 — The conquest win probability is a flat power ratio and can read 50% when the true odds are 0%

```js
function winEstimate(champ, native){                                    // 19836
  const A=champ.stats||battleStats(champ.genome), B=battleStats(native.genome);
  return clamp(A.total/(A.total+B.total), 0.05, 0.95);
}
```

`total` is the unweighted sum of five stats (17472). The estimate ignores abilities, class
innates, the stat *distribution*, and the entire damage formula — even though `vit` alone sets the
HP pool (`×3`) and `fer`/`res` alone drive the exchange. It is rendered as `"…% win"` (19871)
directly beside the warning *"Lose, and a wild-caught champion is lost forever"* (19857).

Measured against the real `runDuel` at identical total power:

| matchup | shown | actual |
|---|---|---|
| `vit90/fer10/res80` vs `vit20/fer80/res20` | 50% | **0%** |
| Cinderburn vs no ability | 50% | **98%** |
| bulwark tank vs striker | 45% | **0%** |

**Fix:** run ~200 seeded `runDuel` calls behind the picker — it is deterministic and cheap — or at
minimum weight by the `vit*3` HP pool and the `fer − 0.45·res` exchange.

### CF1715-10 — "First Arrival" stardust becomes an unbounded faucet after 901 systems

```js
const sysSeen=new Set();                                                // 15409
function _arriveSystem(seed){
  if(_loading || !tutDone || seed==null || seed===SOL_SEED || sysSeen.has(seed)) return;
  sysSeen.add(seed);
  if(sysSeen.size>900){ const _old=sysSeen.values().next().value; sysSeen.delete(_old); }
  essence+=2; …
```

`sysSeen` is the only record of "already paid", and it evicts its oldest member past 900. Once a
player has visited 901 distinct systems, every further arrival evicts an older seed and makes it
payable again. A fixed loop of ~901 charted stars pays +2 ☄ per dive, indefinitely, at a few
seconds each — against a settled world's `6+tier*4` ☄ *per hour*. `stats.arrivals` and
`stats.essenceEarned` inflate with it.

**Fix:** make the cap a hard stop (`if(sysSeen.size>=900) return;` before paying) rather than an
evicting window.

### CF1715-12 — Weekly charters reset on a *backward* clock change

```js
function _chNow(){ return Math.floor(Date.now()/6048e5); }               // 23560
function _chRoll(){ const wk=_chNow(); if(wk!==chWeek){ … } }
```

`!==`, not `>`. Stepping the clock back a week re-rolls the slate. Two charters (`wk-land`

### CF1715-15 — The canvas is focusable and `aria-hidden` at the same time, and it is the first tab stop

```html
<canvas id="cosmos" tabindex="0"></canvas>                              <!-- 1937 -->
```
```js
cv.setAttribute('aria-hidden','true'); cv.setAttribute('role','presentation');   // 2292
```

The v1.7.7 keyboard work is real and good — arrow keys cycle bodies, Enter surveys, a gold ring

### CF1715-19 — Three cosmic-tier gear pieces are strictly dominated by tier-2 and tier-3 gear

The v1.7.13 relic pass fixed the Prismatic Lathe and the Graven Aegis but never checked the v1.7
Forge tier above them, where the ladder runs **backwards**. `_equipBonus` sums one item per slot
(22013), so these are direct comparisons. Dumping every entry in both slots:

```
suit    tier 1  fieldsuit          scut 0.25  land 5
suit    tier 2  hazmat             scut 0.45  land 8
suit    tier 3  thermal/presshull  scut 0.45  land 10  landfam 30
suit    tier 4  cg-proto           scut 0.35  ——            ← below tier 2
suit    tier 5  cg-corona          scut 0.30  ——            ← below tier 4

tool    tier 1  rig1               yield 0.5
tool    tier 2  rig2               yield 1
tool    tier 3  rig3               yield 2     strike 0.04
tool    tier 3  rl-prism           yield 1.5   strike 0.12
tool    tier 5  cg-dark            yield 0.5   strike 0.12   ← same yield as tier 1
```

The Coronal Aegis is the rarest suit in the game and it protects less than the tier-2 Hazmat Suit
while giving up the landing bonus every other suit carries. The Dark Matter Bore has **the same
yield as the starter rig**. These cost Protomatter, Coronium and Dark Matter — materials only the
deepest worlds and star-skims produce.

**Fix:** raise all three above the tier they sit on, and add a boot-time assertion that no
higher-tier item is Pareto-dominated within its own slot. That assertion would have caught this
class of bug before the v1.7.13 pass had to hand-find two instances of it.

### CF1715-20 — The battle log names the wrong fighter as moving first in half of equal-agility duels

### CF1715-21 — Prime Codex titan titles still truncate on phones

`.psub2` got the two-line clamp in v1.7.2; `.pby` (1133) did not, and it carries the claimed
titan's title (23281). At 320–375px the container is ~155px, so "Terrakoth, the Mountain's Fist",
"Zephyrmaw, the Stellar Squall" and "Iridax, the Spectral Paragon" ellipsize — on the game's own
goal screen.

### CF1715-22 — The 44px sweep crushed the Star Atlas name column on small phones

`#log .lfav,.lhome,.del{min-width:44px}` (1926) makes the row's action group `3×44 + 2×3 = 138px`
of `flex:none`. At 320px the sheet is 304px wide; after padding, a 34px thumbnail and the actions,
the name gets **~82px — about 32px when a badge is present**. With `nowrap` + ellipsis (1380),
"Cryohelical Umbraluminant" renders as "Cry…". **Fix:** wrap `.lacts` to a second row under 400px.

### CF1715-23 — On phones the toast lane and the survey card occupy the same box, and the toast now blocks it

`#toast{top:calc(var(--topbar-h,110px) + 8px)}` (1892) and the card's
`_panelTopMin=(W<=900)?(((_tb&&_tb.offsetHeight)||118)+8):64` (13869) are the same expression, and
both are `min(300px, …)` wide. Since v1.7.2 gave toasts `pointer-events:auto` (840) the toast now
**intercepts** the card's ✕ and its primary action row. `_showToast` deliberately exempts the
survey card from board suppression (15462), so nothing prevents the collision.

### CF1715-24 — On desktop the toast overlaps Records and the tray, and the relief is gated on viewport height

`#records` is anchored `bottom:60px` at every height (1874) but the toast's left-lane relief is
`@media (min-width:901px) and (max-height:820px)` (1907). On a 1512×982 laptop the two overlap by
roughly 312×50px, at `pointer-events:auto`. `_showToast`'s suppression only runs when
`innerWidth<=900` (15468), so desktop gets none. **Fix:** drop `and (max-height:820px)` — the left
lane is correct at every desktop height.

## Performance — measured clean, and it is fine

Measured after the fleet finished, on an idle host: both builds served side by side, alternating,
two repetitions each. **v1.7.15 costs essentially nothing in frame rate** despite being 1,209 lines
and 91 KB larger than v1.7.3.

| device | v1.7.3 | v1.7.15 | delta | v1.7.15 jank |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| iPhone 14 (390×844 @3×) | 60.0 | 59.9 | −0.1 | 0.13% |
| Desktop 1080 (1920×1080) | 60.0 | 59.9 | −0.1 | 0.13% |
| iPad Pro 13 (1024×1366 @2×) | 60.0 | 59.3 | −0.7 | 0.30% |
| MacBook Pro 14 (1512×982 @2×) | 58.1 | 56.1 | −2.0 | 0.00% |

The −2.0 on the MacBook profile is inside run-to-run variance — the same build measured 57.3 and
58.1 across the two reps. Jank is at or below 0.3% everywhere. Median cold boot across the
1,000-session fleet was **734 ms**; the instant-link property is intact.

An earlier attempt at this measurement, taken while the fleet was still saturating the host,
produced deltas that flipped sign between repetitions (−4.0, +3.2, −1.6, +1.7). Those numbers are
discarded. Only the idle-host paired figures above are reported.
