# Visual review — midgame atlas and full landfall concepts

**Latest user decision:** after the full-size scenes Nick confirmed the direction “100%” and
asked to bring it to life throughout the game, including universal objects, day/night, seasons
and shareable discoveries. The dated observations below retain concrete implementation and
identity limitations; they do not override his visual-direction approval. See
[TIME_AND_SHARING.md](TIME_AND_SHARING.md) for current source versus the proposed condition recipe.


Agent review of the four retained original PNGs, their complete prompts, the
user's supplied triptych, and the selected canonical Civet image. These are
visual observations, not rendered-pixel identity tests or human acceptance of
every illustration. Nick approved **02's environmental art direction and the
static landing approach**. Approval of 01, 03 and 04 is not recorded here.

## Shared direction worth carrying forward

The environmental paintings make a substantial improvement in cohesion: creature,
plant, rock and water share the same light, local color reflections and atmospheric
depth. Organisms have substrate contact and some foreground overlap. Quieter
distant shapes support readable nearby subjects, so the landscape and discoveries
feel like a single painting. This is the strongest part of the approved direction.

Keep a consistent natural-history fantasy language across every domain: connected
anatomy, whole plant architecture, tactile materials, clear large value groups,
selective fine detail and restrained luminous accents. Cohesion does not require
giving every biome or species the same copper palette. Earth keeps named natural
anatomy, colors and botany; alien forms keep their own data-owned morphology and
palette. Cosmic concepts still need their appropriate physical/form constraints.

## 01 — Discovery atlas

The four-by-three layout communicates fauna, flora and cosmic discovery as one
collection. The natural fox, frond structure, fan plant, veined pods, rosette,
atmospheric world and ringed giant share a detailed material treatment. It is a
useful high-level style overview and is not a sprite or production reference pack.

The six-legged herbivore departs from the requested low-slung body. Its raised
neck, pronounced crest and upright reptilian head read more like a dragon. The
intended gentle, low herbivore silhouette is therefore not locked by this image.
Its visible limb structure needs a separate anatomy pass before six distinct
connected limbs are treated as accepted geometry. The flyer and swimmer also
need their own family-specific joint/fin review before reusable identity claims.

Cosmic panels communicate materials and value hierarchy, not an exact world,
star, comet or orbital state. Their scales and lighting are illustrative. This
does not qualify runtime universe effects, spectral identity or physical motion.

## 02 — Living Worlds triptych

This is the **user-approved visual target** for landfall. The fox belongs to its
wet riverbank through muted sky light, ground contact and overlapping vegetation.
The plateau shares rock bounce and atmospheric distance with its fauna and plants.
The underwater animal takes on the water's color and depth, with a strong
above/below-water environmental composition. Each panel is a scene rather than
a set of isolated portraits placed over a background.

Approval covers that quality and composition strategy. It does not establish
exact canonical planets, ecology, species identities or complete unseen anatomy.
The plateau herbivore changes proportions, crest and apparent body design from
01; the flyer and aquatic animal also vary between illustrations. These should
not be presented as the same exact generated individuals simply because their
roles and palette are similar. Future scene/Compendium/battle assets need an
explicit shared identity recipe and approved complete anatomy.

## 03 — Full Earth landfall

The painting brings the six intended named organisms into one coherent wet
riverbank: Civet, Platypus and Frog; Persimmon, low Cranberry and Devil's Club.
The river leads into layered forest and mountains, while the animals occupy
separate grounded places. Repeated wet-rock, moss and reflected-sky colors connect
the foreground to the vista. Civet feet are on rock, rather than apparently
standing above unbroken open water. The smaller frog and platypus support a
readable encounter composition without making every resident equally large.

The **Civet is not an exact identity match** to
[civet-selected-v1.webp](../CREATURE_SCENE_COHESION_20260908/civet-selected-v1.webp).
Its muzzle is shorter and face rounder/more mask-like; the coat is darker with
larger, more contrasted markings. The body and leg proportions also differ from
the selected long-muzzled, low, deep-bodied Civet. This improves environmental
integration while leaving a specific identity correction unresolved. Do not
replace the selected organism asset or claim Compendium consistency from this
concept alone.

The plant silhouettes are differentiated: fruit-bearing tree on the left, low
red-berried ground cover, and large palmate leaves/red terminal clusters on the
right. **Full botanical review remains required**, including species-specific
leaf arrangement, fruit/calyx, Cranberry runner and leaf scale, and Devil's Club
spines/cluster attachment. The cropped tree crown does not supply a complete
isolated plant portrait. The Platypus's bill, webbing and hidden limb joins also
need close species review before extraction or animation. No claim of natural
geographic co-occurrence is made; these are existing in-game roster names.

## 04 — Full fungal landfall

The three animals occupy different depth/height positions rather than a lineup:
a shelled gastropod on wet foreground stone, a beetle on a root, and a tailed
amphibian on moss. The mushroom canopy, fern curls, divided fronds, moss and
misty rock towers form a coherent habitat. Copper, ivory, slate and green unite
the image while leaving each material distinct. Foot/rock contact and restrained
moist highlights support the approved painted-scene direction.

This is expressly a **fungal-biome family concept**, not a seeded encounter.
The giant fungal forms are environment scenery, not newly defined collectible
flora/fungi. The current family table supports insect/gastropod/amphibian and
moss/fern vocabulary, but it does not determine these particular identities,
numbers, colors, sizes or gameplay properties. The actual source derivation's
contradictory worlds remain in [alien-source](alien-source/README.md).

At native image scale, the amphibian has a more familiar frog/salamander character
than a distinctive data-bound alien design; that is suitable for concept review
but not a finished universal species solution. The beetle's far-leg attachments,
the gastropod's partly obscured underside, and the amphibian's hidden limb joins
cannot be accepted as complete animation geometry from this view. Giant canopy
and small-plant anatomy need their own review if promoted into reusable assets.

## Consequences for implementation

Use these paintings to lock scene cohesion and selective detail, while preserving
complete separate organisms for Compendium and battle reuse. Extracting only
visible pixels cannot recover an occluded foot, opposite-side wing, buried root,
or covered torso surface. A static landfall may be flattened after those shared
assets and their identity/light metadata exist; image generation per runtime seed
is not an implemented procedural pipeline.

This review ran no browser or verification battery, changed no image and closed
no older blocker. Human art acceptance, exact Earth/alien identity qualification,
full botanical review, extraction, native placement, physical phone/Safari/PWA,
all-family motion, memory/heat and the documented full admission chains remain
separate obligations. See the parent [README](README.md) for their retained owners
and the current static-landing direction decision.
