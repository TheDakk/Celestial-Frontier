## Instruments — rebuilt for this round

You said to update the testers, so I rebuilt the harness rather than renaming things.

**18 archetypes**, up from 10:

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| completionist | speedrunner | explorer |
| lore-reader | button-masher | cautious-newbie |
| min-maxer | accessibility | skimmer |
| stress-tester | **hardcore** | **casual** |
| **miner** | **breeder** | **collector** |
| **economist** | **idler** | **backtracker** |

**Why the roster mattered less than the verbs.** The old harness had eight actions — `survey · land · panel · inspect · pan · zoom · keyboard · search` — and every one of them poked the same map. That is why five rounds of testing never reached mid-game, and why my reports have been heavy on onboarding and light on everything after it. Adding persona names to that would have changed nothing.

So I added **twelve goal-directed verbs** driven off the game's own `data-act` / `data-craft` / `data-chacc` hooks:

`mine · harvest · scavenge · tame · conquer · craft · breed · feed · charter · sheet · idle · backout`

with a land-first fallback for the verbs that only exist on the ground, and a vista-dismissal step so landing doesn't strand the bot.

**Three counters per verb, and the middle one is the point:**

- `did` — the verb completed and the world changed
- `saw` — the affordance existed when we looked for it
- `goalFail` — it existed and pressing it changed nothing

`saw` vs `did` is a reachability measure. A system nobody can find is as broken as one that errors, and until this round I had no way to see the difference.

**Mid-game starts.** Half the deep sessions for the eight system-heavy archetypes now boot from a seeded veteran save — 14 fauna, 10 flora, six elements, 9,000 stardust — generated from the build's **own** `makeGenome`, so every codex entry is a valid genome rather than a fabricated stub. That single change is what made the late game testable: before it, `breed` and `craft` did not fire once in a 110-second session.

**Two new instruments beyond the fleet:** the training-reachability sweep now covers 6 viewports and 17 surfaces, and the source-truth voice model was re-extracted from v1.8.5 and re-run over 200,000 genomes.

**Fleet:** 214 sessions · 18 archetypes · 21 device profiles · 144 deep tier. (Planned larger; the twelve new verbs are multi-step and each broad session overran its 13-second budget by 5×, so I traded breadth for the deep tier where the archetype signal actually lives, and added a budget floor so the cheap actions take over below 25 seconds remaining.)

## 1.2 CF1805-01 · The fix's mirror image: the raised board now buries the lesson card · **P1**

Raising the lesson's own surface to `z-index:58` raises it above the **lesson card** at 50.

iPad mini, training step 8 (*"Open a shelf, then tap a specimen"*):

```
#codex    z=58  rect=72,611,600,380   reachable=100%
#tutbox   z=50  rect=182,824,380,139  reachable=  0%   #codex 63/63
```

The screenshot is in the bundle. There is no "Field Training 8 / 21" card anywhere on screen — the instruction and its Skip button are both behind the Compendium. Same on iPad Air. The bands overlap completely: the codex spans y 611–991, the card 824–963.

**Root cause is one missing piece of CSS, not the z-index.** The training layout contract covers `#panel` only:

```css
body.training #panel{top:calc(var(--tut-bot,150px) + 8px) !important; ...}   /* 1286 */
body.training #panel{max-height:min(var(--tut-cap,60dvh), ...)}             /* 1795 */
```

`#log`, `#codex`, `#chpanel` and `#records` are all now raisable to 58 and **none of them gets `--tut-bot` positioning**, so instead of rendering below the card they render through it. That is also why `#log` is still 14% reachable at step 6, unchanged from v1.8.2.

**Fix:** extend the positioning contract to every surface in `TUT_PRI_SURF`. Then a raised board sits below the card the way the panel does, and the card can safely outrank everything. The alternative — making `overtop` follow the raise — re-races the z-index instead of fixing the geometry, and it will hide the bottom third of the raised board.

## 1.3 CF1805-02 · Duel XP: the guard was fixed and the payout was not · **high**

This one is worth reading closely, because the code documents the diagnosis and then does not act on it.

```js
/* ROUND 7 CF1802-18: mine.id is set at no reachable call site — derive
   the catalogue identity from the genome instead, so participation XP
   stops depending on each caller remembering a field. */
const _mid=(mine && (mine.id || (mine.genome && !mine.isPlayer ? codexId(mine.genome) : null)));
if(res.winner!=='A' && _mid && codex.has(_mid)){
  const _dl=Date.now();
  if(_dl-_lastDuelPart>30000){ _lastDuelPart=_dl;
    const _close=(res.hpB/Math.max(1,res.maxB))<0.25;
    awardXP(mine.id, _close?3:2, ...); }        // ← still mine.id
}
```

`_mid` is derived correctly and used in the guard. The award is still `mine.id`.

The block sits inside the `else` of `if(opts.onResolve)`, so it is reached only from the two friendly-duel call sites, and both build `{name, genome, art}` with no `id`. `awardXP(undefined, …)` hits `codex.get(undefined)` and returns.

**"A bout survived" (+2) and "a fight taken to the wire" (+3) still pay nothing** — and it is now strictly worse than before, because the guard fires, so `_lastDuelPart` is consumed and the next qualifying bout inside 30 seconds is blocked too. Previously the guard never fired and the throttle was never touched.

**And the bigger one, adjacent and unfixed:**

```js
if(_dn-_lastDuelCredit>30000){ _lastDuelCredit=_dn; stats.duelwins++;
  if(mine && mine.id) awardXP(mine.id, 8, 'a duel won'); }
```

`mine.id` is undefined at every reachable call site on this path, so **the +8 duel-win award has never paid, in any build.** `stats.duelwins++` is outside the guard, so the win counts toward rank and achievements while the creature gets nothing. The Guide advertises it at line 19309.

+8 is the largest single XP award in the game — larger than the +5 lineage bonus. Fix is one identifier in three places: pass `_mid`.

## 1.4 CF1805-03 · Five new wrong-modulus bugs, in the same function that fixed one · **medium**

CF1802-21 is **fixed, correctly**: `const bold=_TEMPER_BOLD[((+g.temper||0)%FA_TEMPER.length)]` — the right gene, the right modulus, `_TEMPER_BOLD` and `FA_TEMPER` both exactly 10 entries, indexed the same way the card is.

Three lines below it, CF1802-20's fix folds four more genes in:

```js
const _vw=(v,n,amt)=>1+(((((+v||0)%n)/(n-1))-0.5)*amt);
const fTrait=_vw(g.trait,7,0.20), fBody=_vw(g.body,9,0.14),
      fLoco=_vw(g.loco,6,0.18),  fDiet=_vw(g.diet,5,0.22),
      fSense=_vw(g.sense,6,0.12);
```

| gene | modulus used | actual length | |
|---|---|---|---|
| `g.trait` | 7 | `FA_TRAIT` = **25** | ✗ |
| `g.body` | 9 | `FA_BODY` = **16** | ✗ |
| `g.loco` | 6 | `FA_LOCO` = **18** | ✗ |
| `g.diet` | 5 | `FA_DIET` = **6** | ✗ |
| `g.sense` | 6 | `FA_SENSE` = **10** | ✗ |

Five for five. The correct idiom — `%FA_TEMPER.length` — is literally three lines above.

The vocabulary genuinely widens, so CF1802-20 is substantially fixed. But the mapping is not a clean traversal: `g.diet` has 6 values and `%5` makes omnivore sound identical to herbivore; `g.trait` collapses 25 traits onto 7 buckets non-uniformly, so traits 0–3 are four times as common in the voice as traits 4–6. Change five numbers to `FA_X.length`.

## 1.5 CF1805-04 · The quest log loses its handle exactly when a stuck player needs it · **medium**

```js
if(questLogOpen){ questLogOpen=false; renderQuestLog(); if(!go) return; }
else if(!go){ questLogOpen=true; renderQuestLog(); return; }   /* the chip is the log's handle */
```

`questLogOpen=true` is assigned in exactly one place, and it requires `go` to be falsy. CF1802-04's fix replaced `_nextBest()`'s only `go:null` return with `go:'cosmos'` — so **every** path now returns a truthy `go`, and `el.dataset.go` is always truthy while stalled.

Net effect: while the stall suggestion is showing, pressing the chip can never open the quest log. It can only close one already open. The quest log is a v1.8.1 headline feature — "the objective chip is now a handle" — and it loses that handle in precisely the state where a player would want to check what they are supposed to be doing.

## 1.6 CF1805-05 · Harvest: the in-session gate is closed, the reload path is not · **high**

CF1802-14's fix adds a monotonic clock check:

```js
/* ROUND 7 CF1802-14: perfTime() is monotonic from boot — the OS clock is not.
   An in-session harvest must satisfy BOTH, so winding the device clock forward
   buys nothing. In-memory only: the load path's _hvFloor already covers reloads,
   so this needs no save-shape change. */
const _hvMono=new Map();
```

The first half is true and well done — winding the clock forward inside a session now buys nothing. The last sentence is not true.

`_hvMono` is a plain in-memory Map, never persisted. A reload empties it, so the monotonic gate is skipped entirely and only the wall-clock check `now - c.t < HARVEST_CD` applies. And `_hvFloor = Math.max(0, _atL - HARVEST_CD)` floors the stamp **upward** — it defends against a *lowered* stamp, which is the save-edit case its own comment describes. It does nothing about an *inflated* `Date.now()`.

**Sequence:** harvest → wind the clock forward one hour → reload → harvest again. No save editing, no dev tools.

Earth alone yields 26 ☄ per cycle at ~15 seconds a cycle — roughly **6,200 ☄/hour against 26 ☄/hour by design, a 240× rate.** For scale, the entire stardust economy (all six techs at 580 ☄ plus every ship system at 320 ☄) is about 900 ☄, so nine minutes of the loop buys everything purchasable.

**Fix:** persist the monotonic stamps, or floor `conq.t` on load using a monotonic quantity rather than a wall-clock one.

## 1.7 CF1805-06 · `size` is the one linear power term nothing clamps · **medium (save-edit scope)**

CF1802-15's fix mirrors `normGenome`'s handling of *battlefield modifiers* — `delete g._mult; delete g._wf;` and a range check on `apex`/`par`. Correct, and it closes the 1000× champion.

It does not mirror `normGenome`'s coercion of the **24 trait indices**, and one of those is a linear power term:

```js
const sz=(g.size||0);
s[0]+=sz*4; s[3]=Math.max(6, s[3]-sz*2);
```

Legitimate range is 0–5. `_sanitizeSavedGenome` clamps `brood`, `fed`, `xp`, `hurt`, `_mult`, `_wf`, `apex`, `par` — precisely the fields that were exploited before — and never touches `size`. Editing one codex entry's `size` to 1,000,000 gives +4,000,000 vitality, and `runDuel` decides a capped fight on HP *fraction*, so a 12-million-HP pool cannot be dented in 26 rounds.

It also travels: `encodeCreature` serialises the raw genome and `normGenome` does `Math.abs((+o.size)|0)`, which preserves 1e6 intact — so a crafted share code presents a four-million-power challenger in another player's duel box.

**There is a live, non-exploit consequence too.** `crossGenome`'s mutation list includes `size` and never wraps it, so size legitimately drifts above 5 in normal play (mean-reverting to about 3.3, +0.39 per generation under bracket-breeding). The card renders `pick(FA_SIZE, g.size)`, which is mod 6, while `battleStats` uses it raw — **so a size-9 creature reads "dog-sized" and fights with +36 vitality.** That divergence is worth closing on its own.

## 1.8 CF1805-07 · A forward clock still re-rolls the weekly charter slate · **medium**

`_chArmed` correctly defers `_chRoll` past the boot tick, which closes CF1720-06. It does not limit how often the roll can happen afterward.

`_chRoll` clears every weekly `chProg` key and every weekly `chacc` entry when `wk > chWeek`, and `_chDoneOf` for a weekly is `(chProg[c.id]||0) >= c.n` — so deleting those keys makes **every already-completed weekly claimable again**, and `_chWeekly()` reseeds a fresh slate. Any board render triggers it.

Measured over 200 forward week-steps from the real current week: every pool id recurs in 33–42% of weeks, and the average claimable value per step is **77.5 ☄ — the same figure the design intends per real week.**

Credit where it is due: CF1802-13's instant-claim half **is** fixed. The landfall-banking replay is now explicitly `id[0]!=='w'`, so a rolled weekly cannot self-complete on the accept click. The deeds still have to be done. What is left is the cadence: 30–80 ☄ per one to three minutes instead of per week.

# 3 · The path to 10/10

Bugs are the floor, not the ceiling. Here is what I think actually stands between this build and a 10, in the order I would spend the effort.

## 3.1 Nobody finishes Field Training, and that is the single biggest structural issue

The numbers across eight rounds, all from the same unchanged instrument:

- **50%** of sessions skip training outright.
- **100%** of rage quits (all four rounds where I measured them) skipped training.
- My bots have never completed all 21 steps — 0% in six consecutive rounds — and while that is a harness limit, the *stall distribution* is real and it has always clustered in the first third.
- Rage quits have risen for four builds: 3 → 5 → 7 → 10.

Twenty-one steps is a lot to ask before the game starts. The steps themselves are well written and each one teaches something real. But the shape — a linear 21-gate corridor that owns the screen — is fighting the game it is introducing, which is an open sandbox whose whole pitch is that you wander and find things.

**What I would try:** cut the mandatory path to the five steps that unlock a loop — find a world, chart it, land, catch something, look at it in the Compendium — and turn the other sixteen into *contextual* one-liners that fire the first time a player opens the surface they describe. The stall detector already has the machinery for this; it just never gets to use it because the objective chip returns early for exactly the players who need it (CF1802-03 fixed that, this round, which is real progress).

That change is also the highest-leverage thing available for the rage-quit trend, because the population that quits and the population that skips training are the same population.

## 3.2 Make the rewards you advertise actually pay

Across two rounds, the running tally of the eight broadened-XP awards:

| award | status |
|---|---|
| welcome meal +1 | ✓ fixed and ledgered this round |
| taste discovered +2 | ✓ always worked |
| successful union +2 | ✓ fixed this round |
| first-of-its-kind lineage +5 | ⚠ pays, but on **every** breed |
| bout survived +2 | ✗ still dead (CF1805-02) |
| fight taken to the wire +3 | ✗ still dead (CF1805-02) |
| conquest lost +3 | ✓ fixed this round |
| defender nearly broken +5 | ✓ fixed, though foreclosed by an earlier +3 |
| **duel won +8** | ✗ **never paid in any build** |

That is five of nine now correct, up from four dead. The remaining three are one identifier — pass `_mid` instead of `mine.id`.

I would treat this as a category, not three tickets. Every reward the Guide advertises deserves a test that asserts the XP arrived, not that the code path ran. That is the same recommendation I have made for five rounds, and it is the one that would have caught all three of these at once.

## 3.3 Close the audio character gap while it is still cheap

The synthesis architecture is genuinely good and I want to be clear about that: procedural, deterministic across independent page loads, zero assets, correctly gated by two persisted toggles, damage-scaled in combat, and the Earth taxonomy really does give a wolf a roar and a rattlesnake a hiss. CF1802-19 — the bed that kept playing after Sound → Off — is fixed.

What is left is character resolution, and it is five numbers (CF1805-03) plus one design question: `_blendVoice` flips the voice's *kind* at anchor < 0.5, but the renderer keeps grafting onto the Earth rig for any `_earthBlend` at all. At anchor 0.46 a creature still looks like a wolf and already sounds like an insect. If sound and vision are meant to drift together, tie the kind flip to the same threshold the renderer uses.

Then run a human listening test. My fleet is structurally deaf — Playwright runs muted and no persona reads an audio signal — so a flat result from me would be meaningless, and I am not going to dress one up. But the architecture is now sound enough that a listening test would produce a real answer, which is exactly what §15 needs before anyone commits 10–18 weeks.

## 3.4 Finish the feedback grammar

`playConfirm` is now called from three real press paths — that is CF1802-22 closed, and the deny tone now fires from presses rather than from a markup builder. Good.

The gap that remains is coverage: `_denial` handles 3 modes and there are roughly 24 refusal paths. The two worth doing next are the ones where there is nothing to press at all — the unaffordable Build/tech button, which is not rendered, and any refusal that currently returns silently. A player who presses something and gets *nothing* — no motion, no sound, no words — concludes the game is broken. A player who gets a two-line explanation and a button that goes somewhere concludes the game is deep. Same code path, opposite outcome.

## 3.5 Make the mid-game as reachable as the systems are good

This is the observation I could not make before this round, because my harness could not get there either.

The systems past the first hour — breeding lineages, the Fabricator chain, conquest, the ascent — are the best part of this game. They are also the part almost nothing reaches. I had to seed a mid-game save to test them at all. A player faces the same wall from the other side: a full cargo hold makes about ten of 62 recipes, the folds that would show them the chain start closed, and the verbs that drive the deep loops (`mine`, `harvest`, `conquer`, `scavenge`, `tame`) only exist on a world you have already landed on.

Three cheap moves, in order of value:

1. **A "makeable now" filter in the Fabricator**, plus a two-step chain hint on gated recipes ("needs 2× Plate — you have the iron"). The data is already there; `_craftNeed` computes it for the button face.
2. **Open the fold that contains something actionable.** "Nothing auto-opens" was the right fix for the training steps that relied on a pre-opened drawer; applied globally it now hides every affordance behind an extra tap, and the Compendium re-collapses after every specimen view (CF1802-08).
3. **Surface one mid-game goal early.** The Ascent chapters and charters both do this well when they are visible; the objective chip is the natural place, and it now renders for objective-less players.

## 3.6 What is already 10/10, and should not be touched

Worth saying plainly, because eight rounds of fix lists can read as if nothing works.

- **Stability.** Zero uncaught errors, zero unhandled rejections, zero storage failures, zero stuck overlays, zero fatals — across roughly 6,200 sessions and six builds. That is unusual, and it is the hardest thing on this list to keep.
- **Determinism.** Voices, portraits, world generation and duel outcomes are all reproducible from a seed, and the fingerprint gate has held through every round.
- **Payload and boot.** 653 KB gzipped for an entire procedural universe with procedural audio, and the instant-link property intact. Cold boot cost of the whole v1.8 audio arc measured at +8 ms.
- **The writing.** Every denial, every card, every step of training reads like someone cared. That is the hardest thing on this list to buy and it is already done.
- **The fix discipline this round.** Fifteen of twenty-five, both prior partials closed, and the two biggest — the mobile training wall and the free-catalogue mint — closed properly, with comments that reason about *why* rather than just noting what changed.

# 4 · The pattern, round 8

Rounds 2–5 shipped fixes into places they could not run. Round 6 shipped fixes that ran with the wrong value. Round 7 shipped fixes that ran everywhere instead of only where needed.

**Round 8's variant: fixes that correctly diagnose the problem in a comment, and then don't act on the diagnosis.**

- **CF1805-02** — "`mine.id` is set at no reachable call site", says the comment, immediately above code that passes `mine.id`.
- **CF1805-05** — "the load path's `_hvFloor` already covers reloads", says the comment, about a floor that only clamps in the other direction.
- **CF1805-03** — the correct modulus idiom sits three lines above five incorrect ones.
- **CF1805-01** — the fix scoped the raise correctly and did not notice that the lesson card was also below it.

Every one is a case where the reasoning was right and the last mile was not checked. That is a much better failure mode than the previous seven rounds, and it points at exactly one remedy:

> **Assert the outcome, in the units the player experiences.** Not "the guard fires" — *the XP arrived*. Not "the mono gate exists" — *the second harvest is refused after a reload*. Not "the surface is raised" — *the instruction is readable*.

Three of the four items above would have been caught by a test that reads the save after the action instead of checking that the branch was taken. That is a smaller investment than the fixes themselves.
