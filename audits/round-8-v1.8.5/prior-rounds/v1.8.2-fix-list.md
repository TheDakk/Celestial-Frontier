# P0 — the mobile training wall

## CF1802-01 · Training raises the survey card above the entire dock · **P0 · phones and tablets only**

**One rule, line 1841:**

```css
body.training:not(.vista) #panel{z-index:58}
```

`#codexbtn` and `#logbtn` sit at `z-index:14` on phones (`z-index:8` on desktop). `#codex` and `#log` sit at `z-index:22`. Under 900px, lines 1528–1529 give `#panel`, `#codex` and `#log` the same width and the same right offset, so they occupy an identical band. The panel wins outright.

**Player consequence.** Step 5 says *"Open the Star Atlas (the dock)"* — only the right sliver of the button is pressable, and the Atlas that opens is a strip above the card. Step 7 says *"open your Compendium to meet them"* — that button cannot be pressed at all. That is a hard stop, and it is the one Nick hit.

**Corroborated independently by the fleet.** Across the last four rounds the bots stalled only at training steps 2 and 7. In v1.8.2, **steps 5 and 8 appear as stall points for the first time** — `atlas-open` and `specimen`, precisely the two steps whose lesson surface is a board:

**Recommended fix — the pattern is already in the file.** Two hundred lines away, `_tutShow` does exactly the right thing for the training card:

```js
tutEl.classList.toggle('overtop', !!(s && s.id==='sheet'));
```

```js

  !!(s && (s.id==='survey-tour' || s.id==='atlas-add' || s.id==='land')));
```
```css
/* line 1841 */
body.training.tut-panel:not(.vista) #panel{z-index:58}
```

Those are the only three steps whose lesson surface *is* the survey card.

**Regression assert worth adding.** For each training step, `document.elementFromPoint` at the centre of that step's own `spot` target must resolve inside that target. That single check converts "the rule shipped" into "the thing the lesson points at is pressable" — which is the exact gap that has bitten five of the last six rounds.

# Review 1 — player experience

**1,000 sessions, 10 personas, 21 device profiles.** 1000/1000 ok. 0 uncaught errors. 0 rejections. 0 storage failures. 0 stuck overlays. 0 fatals. fps median 34.8 (p10 17.9), boot median 581 ms.

## CF1802-03 · Rage quits rose for the fourth build running, and the feature aimed at them cannot reach the players who quit · **high**

Answering the reviewer's ask #3 directly.

| build | n | rage quits | rate /1000 | 95% CI on the count |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| v1.7.15 | 1000 | 8 | 8.0 | 3.4 – 15.8 |
| v1.7.18 | 1000 | 3 | 3.0 | 0.6 – 8.8 |
| v1.7.19 | 1000 | 5 | 5.0 | 1.6 – 11.7 |
| v1.7.20 | 1000 | 7 | 7.0 | 2.8 – 14.4 |
| **v1.8.2** | **1000** | **10** | **10.0** | **4.8 – 18.4** |

**Stated honestly: v1.7.20 → v1.8.2 is z = 1.06 — not distinguishable from noise.** No single step in this series is significant. What is not noise is the direction: four consecutive builds, 3 → 5 → 7 → 10, and v1.8 was aimed squarely at reversing it.

**And there is a mechanism, which is the useful part.** Every one of the 10 rage quits skipped training. The fleet-wide skip rate is 50%, so 10/10 is p ≈ 0.001. They are concentrated entirely in the impatient personas — speedrunner 4, button-masher 3, skimmer 3; the other seven personas produced zero.

The stall detector is supposed to catch exactly this. It cannot, because of the guard at line 24458:

```js
if(!g){ if(el) el.style.display='none'; _chipLast=null; return; }
...
const _nb=(_stall>=10)?_nextBest():null;   // ← never reached when g is null
```

`g` is the current objective — an accepted charter or an active Ascent chapter goal. **A player who skipped training and has not accepted a charter has no `g`, so `renderChip` returns before the stall branch and the suggestion never renders.** That population is 50% of the fleet and 100% of the rage quits.

Fix: hoist the stall branch above the `if(!g)` return, so a player with no objective at all — the player most in need of a nudge — is the one who gets it.

## CF1802-04 · The stall detector is not cost-ordered and does suggest the unavailable · **medium**

Two claims in §3 item 3 do not hold against `_nextBest` (24740–24760):

- **"ordered by cost"** — it is a fixed if-chain, first match wins, with no numeric cost read anywhere in the function. Crafting (spends materials *and* stardust) is ranked above landing and scavenging (spends one bio attempt). It also returns one suggestion, never a list.
- **"never suggesting the unavailable"** — steps 1 and 2 are guarded (`_chAvailable()`, `_canCraft(it)`). **Step 3 has no guard at all:** `if(!flora) return {t:'Land on a living world and Scavenge for flora', go:'log'}`. Step 4 below it checks `logMap.size`; step 3 does not. A player with an empty Star Atlas gets told to land on a living world, presses the CTA, and arrives at *"Nothing saved yet."*

The function's own header comment is `/* the best action AVAILABLE NOW — never suggest what the player cannot do */`, sitting directly above the unguarded branch.

Also: step 5 returns `go:null`, which becomes `dataset.go=''`, which the click handler treats as "no destination" and **toggles the quest log instead** — the chip says "Survey a world and press Land" and opens the quest log.

## CF1802-05 · `_PROGRESS_EVT` contains an event name the game never emits · **medium**

The progress set (24736) contains `'skim'`. The game emits `'skimmed'` (20882). **Skim a star's corona ten times in a row and the objective chip flips to stall mode and suggests you go do something else, while you are actively mining stardust.** One-character fix.

## CF1802-06 · Quest-log progress is a snapshot, not live · **medium**

`renderQuestLog` has exactly two call sites: the chip click (24464) and `ascEvent` after an Ascent goal ticks (24732). It is not called from `_chBadge`, `charterEvent`, `_chComplete` or `gameEvent`, and there is no interval or observer.

Open the quest log, then feed a beast toward a "Feed 5" charter: the chip bumps, and the charter row in the log stays frozen at its old count with a bar that does not move. Accept a charter while the log is open and no row appears. The one thing the log adds over the chip — per-charter progress — is the thing that does not refresh.

Two related wiring defects: while stalled, `dataset.go` is truthy so the chip click takes a destination branch and **can no longer close the log** (and `#questlog` is not in the Escape stack); and when the chip hides for want of an objective (24458) the log is left on screen with no handle and no way out.

## CF1802-07 · The denial contract covers 3 of ~24 refusal paths · **medium**

§3 item 1 says *"Every 'can't' names what is missing, why, and where to get it, with a button that goes there."* `_denial` handles exactly three modes — `breed`, `feed`, `heal` — and `_denialHTML` is called from one place (18763, the empty-picker branch of `openPicker`).

The three covered paths are genuinely good, and the shortfall-on-the-button-face claim is true (15007/15010 render `esc(d.short)` with `class="needs"`). The other twenty-one are not covered. The two that matter most:

- **Craft, can't afford** (22438): `<button class="bclaim need" aria-disabled="true" data-tip="…">Need 2× Iron</button>` — **no `data-craft`**, so the yard delegate's `closest('[data-craft]')` misses it entirely. Press it and nothing happens: no panel, no toast, no sound. On desktop a hover tooltip repeats what the button face already says; on touch there is no hover at all, so it is simply a dead button that names a gap and offers no way to close it.
- **Research/Build tech, can't afford** (22459): `(afford?'<button…>Build</button>':'')` — there is no element at all, only a red cost line. Nothing to press.

And three paths are **silent returns** with no feedback of any kind: any card verb during Field Training (14118–14122), any reveal-card verb during training (15030–15033), and `chAccept` during training (24333–24334). During the atlas-add step, tapping 🐾 Tame on Earth's card does nothing — `_tutGate` lets the click through because `#panel` is in `s.allow`, and it is then dropped silently.

# Review 2 — bugs and exploits

Ranked worst first. Every item below was traced to a concrete repeatable action sequence and then actively attacked for a guard; the ones that had a guard were dropped and are listed at the end.

## CF1802-09 · Tapping a life-form row catalogues an uncaught species for free · **high · source-confirmed, not yet observed live**

The world card's Life-forms fold lists **all** species on the world, caught or not — line 13835 counts `spRows.filter(row=>codex.has(...))` and the digest prints *"X / N in your Compendium — tame and scavenge the rest."* Every row carries `data-sp`. The handler at 14186:

```js
const sp=e.target.closest('[data-sp]');
if(sp && _lastDesc && _lastDesc.species){
  const g=_lastDesc.species.find(x=>x.seed===+sp.dataset.sp);
  if(g){ const entry=codex.get(codexId(g)) || _storeSpecies(g, _lastDesc.planetName||_lastDesc.title, _lastDesc.where);
```

The `|| _storeSpecies(...)` is the whole finding: tapping the row of a species you have **not** caught mints it into the Compendium. It calls neither `tryCapture` (which spends a finite `bioLeft` attempt) nor `captureOdds` (whose `TAME_ODDS` bottoms out at 0.0025 for tier 14). `_storeSpecies` refuses only `if(codex.has(id))` — so feeding the specimen away and tapping the row again re-mints it. Roughly one specimen per second, unbounded.

`ringGrade` is still applied, so this bypasses *acquisition*, not the rarity spectrum. But it is the supply line for CF1802-10, -11 and -12 below.

**Honesty note:** this is source-verified along an unambiguous code path, but I could not confirm it at runtime — my open-play landing driver failed to reach a living world in the time available. **Twenty seconds on your side would settle it:** land on a living world, press Discover Life, unfold the roster, and tap a row for a species you have *not* caught. If it appears in your Compendium, it is confirmed.

## CF1802-10 · "A welcome meal" has no ledger — `fed` and creature XP are unbounded · **high · confirmed**

`feedPair` line 18665:

```js
if(pref==='loved'){ awardXP(_fid, 1, 'a welcome meal');
  awardXPOnce(_fid, 'taste:'+ftype, 2, 'a taste discovered'); }
```

The +2 is ledgered through `awardXPOnce`/`xpFirsts`. **The +1 is a bare `awardXP` with no key, no ledger, no cooldown.** `fed` increments at 18647 with no cap. `battleStats` 17757:

```js
const bonusRaw=Math.min(200,(g.brood||0))*22 + Math.min(200,(g.fed||0))*10;
```

`fed=100` → +1000 power; `fed=200` → +1222. A tier-14 Apex Guardian's entire base budget is ~717. A loved meal averages +2.25 `fed`, so ~89 meals takes any creature to the ceiling. With CF1802-09 supplying free flora, that is minutes of tapping. The same loop maxes the *player's* permanent stats through `healExplorer` (19019–19047), whose poison branch is explicitly capped non-lethal at 19038 — so there is no failure mode with a real cost.

## CF1802-11 · A lost conquest is never recorded; the same world pays XP forever · **high · confirmed**

Award at 20592–20595. `conquered.set` happens **only on a win** (20601). `conquerPlanet` (20529) blocks only `conquered.has(...)`, and the button re-renders whenever the world is unconquered (13901). Nothing anywhere writes a loss.

The real guard — a bred champion is pinned to `hurt=0.85` on loss and deleted on a second loss while still ≥0.85 (20679–20690) — is defeated by one meal: `feedPair` mends 0.10 for a neutral flora and 0.22+ for a loved one. +3 to +5 XP per ~15 s cycle, on one planet, without moving.

The comment at 20590 — *"Never a farm: one world can only be attempted meaningfully while it stays unconquered"* — has it backwards. **Losing is what keeps it unconquered.**

## CF1802-12 · Free mates turn grade-uncapped breeding into a rarity slot machine · **medium-high**

`breedPair` stores the child with `where=null`, and `gradeCapAt(null)` returns `TIER_MAX` — bred children ignore the ring spectrum by design. `speciesGrade` then stacks up to +5 boost onto `rarityRoll`. The designed brake is that a failed pairing destroys both parents; with CF1802-09 a mate costs one tap.

Simulated with the real `crossGenome`/`evolveGenome`/`speciesGrade`/`rarityRoll`/`breedOdds`, 200k attempts, total loss on failure:

| | no stardust | +15% stardust | intended per-roll rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| tier ≥ 8 (Mythic) | 1 per 64 | 1 per 41 | — |
| tier ≥ 10 (Primordial) | 1 per 683 | 1 per 415 | 1 in 333,000 |
| tier 12 | reached | reached | 1 in 3.3M |

A Primordial in one to two hours of clicking.

## CF1802-13 · Weekly charters re-roll on every forward clock step, and landfall charters then self-complete · **medium**

`_chRoll` (24255–24263) clears every weekly `chProg` key and `chacc` entry when `wk>chWeek`; `_chWeekly` reseeds off `chWeek`. `chAccept` (24345–24352) then replays the **persisted** `landed` set through the charter's filter, so `wk-land` (n:3) and `wk-hostile` (n:1) complete on the accept click.

Set the clock +7 days, open the Charters board (any render calls `_chRoll`), accept, get paid, repeat. Measured over 200 consecutive week buckets: 67% of weeks contain at least one landfall charter; mean instantly-claimable value **20.8 ☄ per clock step** against a designed 78 ☄ per real week. ~60 ☄/min.

The backward-clock re-roll *was* correctly fixed (`wk>chWeek`, not `!==`). This is the forward direction.

### CF1802-13b · CF1720-06 is only half fixed

The `chWeek` repair is indeed gone from `loadSave` (16648) and now lives at 24257. **But `_chRoll` still runs inside `loadSave` on the same boot tick**: 16718 `_chBadge()` → 24445 `if(tutDone){ const acc=_chAccepted();` → 24299 `_chRoll()`. For every save with `tutDone === true` — every returning player — the clamp is evaluated against exactly the pre-NTP `Date.now()` the fix's rationale calls untrustworthy. Concrete: save holds `chw:2900`, device clock lags 3 weeks at boot, load-time `_chRoll` fires `2900 > 2898` → `chWeek=2897`; when the clock corrects, the next roll takes `wk>chWeek` and wipes every in-progress weekly — the exact outcome the fix claims to prevent.

## CF1802-14 · Harvest cooldown is checked against the live clock only · **medium**

`doHarvest` 20704–20712, `HARVEST_CD = 3600e3`. The **load** path is properly hardened (`_hvFloor=Math.max(0,_atL-HARVEST_CD)` at 16490, `_atL` clamped ≤ now at 16483), so a save edit buys exactly one cycle. It simply does not cover a running session: advance the OS clock one hour and every settled world is ready at once. Thirty tier-8 worlds ≈ 1,140 ☄ per step, a ~3,600× speedup.

## CF1802-15 · `_sanitizeSavedGenome` clamps four fields and misses the three that matter · **medium**

16449–16460 clamps `brood`, `fed`, `xp`, `hurt` and nothing else. It does not touch `_mult`, `_wf` or `apex`, all of which round-trip because the codex is written wholesale at 16428. `battleStats` 17761 does `if(g._mult && g._mult>1) s[i]=Math.round(s[i]*g._mult)` with no bound; `abilityOf` grants `apex` the Sovereign ability at max magnitude plus 10% damage reduction, and `speciesGrade` forces tier 12–14.

The share-code path validates all three (`normGenome` deletes `_mult`/`_wf`, range-checks `apex`) and the guardian-capture path deletes them — which is exactly why an edited localStorage save yields a 1000× champion for free. The sanitiser should mirror `normGenome`.

## CF1802-16 · Two of the eight XP awards are paid to creatures that are then deleted · **medium**

`breedPair` 18568–18569 pays `awardXP(aEntry.id, 2, 'a successful union')` and `awardXPOnce(aEntry.id, 'lineage:…', 5, …)`, then 18581 does `removeFromCodex(aEntry.id); removeFromCodex(bEntry.id)`. `crossGenome` never copies `xp`, so the child inherits none of it.

**"A successful union" (+2) and "a first-of-its-kind lineage" (+5) are unreachable by the player** — two of the eight rewards in the §3 item 2 table. The `xpFirsts` entry written at 18569 is dead weight keyed to a deleted id.

## CF1802-17 · Breeding silently destroys the `fed` investment · **medium**

`breedPair` 18574 sums `brood` across the parents. `crossGenome`'s field set has no `fed`. So a hybrid of two well-fed parents starts at `fed=0` — and per CF1802-10's arithmetic that is up to 2,000 power thrown away with no warning anywhere in the UI.

**This is also why the v1.8 breeding preview overstates.** The band at 18774 is `lo=min(A.total,B.total)*0.95`, `hi=max(A.total,B.total)*1.18`, where `A.total`/`B.total` come from `battleStats`, which **includes** the `fed` bonus the child will not inherit. Worst case measured last round: **6.2× overstated**. §3 item 5 promises "ranges only, never the roll" — the range itself is wrong.

## CF1802-18 · Two v1.8 outcome lines are dead · **confirmed, carried from last round**

`if(res.winner!=='A' && mine && mine.id)` at 18148 is never true: `fightNow(mine, theirs, opts)` is called from 18221 (`_duelMine={name,genome,art}`), 20578 and 25166 (`{name,genome,art}`) — **no `id` at any reachable call site.** "Bout survived" (+2) and "fight taken to the wire" (+3) never pay. Together with CF1802-16 that is **four of the eight rewards** in the broadened-XP table that cannot be earned.

# Review 3 — audio

**The first measurement this project has had.** Method: a `Proxy` on the `AudioContext` constructor wrapping `createOscillator`/`createGain`/`createBiquadFilter`/`createBufferSource`, with `setValueAtTime` and the ramp methods patched, so every voice becomes a comparable parameter fingerprint and every `start`/`stop` is timestamped. Separately, `voiceOf`, `_VOICE`, `_blendVoice`, `hashInt`, `makeGenome`, `crossGenome` and `_earthArt` were extracted **verbatim** from the build by brace-matching and run over 200,000 genomes.

## What is genuinely good, and measured

- **Determinism holds.** The same genome produces a byte-identical parameter set across two independent page loads. ✓
- **The toggles work and persist.** With Creature voices off, zero oscillators are created — the gate is real, not a volume trim. Both toggles round-trip through the save (`vce`/`cbx` at 16388, restored at 16639–16640, absent ⇒ on). ✓
- **Pitch tracks the size the card shows, exactly.** `sizeF=1/(0.55+(g.size%6)*0.22)` uses the same modulus as `FA_SIZE` and `FA_SIZE_M`, so tiny → 363 Hz through titanic → 121 Hz, monotone, matching the label. I checked this specifically because `%6` against an unbounded mutating gene looked like a bug; it is not. ✓
- **Every rig family has a voice.** All 17 named archetypes appear in `_earthArt` recipes across 46 recipes. Wolf → mammal/roar 148 Hz · Sparrow → bird/chirp 2068 Hz · Blue Whale → marine/song 187 Hz, 1.20 s · Rattlesnake → serpent/hiss 630 Hz, nz 0.95. The four named claims in §2 all check out. ✓
- **Combat damage scaling is real.** `playHit(L.dmg/Math.max(1,_mx), !!L.crit, !!(L.fs||L.ex||L.stp))` at 18173 — `frac` is the blow's damage as a fraction of the target's max HP, and it drives the body thump (`220-heavy*120` Hz), the impact band (`900+heavy*700`), the envelope and the tail. Crits get a separate 1760→2640 Hz ring; ability procs a 330→880 Hz saw through a Q-4 bandpass. Skipped playback is genuinely gated (`!_skip`). ✓
- **No audio assets ship.** Zero references to `.mp3`/`.ogg`/`.wav`/`.m4a`, zero `decodeAudioData`. "Never a sample" holds literally. ✓

## CF1802-19 · The bed keeps playing after the player switches Sound off · **high · the reviewer's ask #5, measured**

§2 claims the ambience *"stops on close, on hidden tab and on sound-off."* Two of three hold. Measured, with every node instrumented:

```
A. planetfall       : loops=1  live=1   loop#105 started@14075 stopped=NEVER
B. Sound toggle On -> Off
   after sound OFF  : loops=1  live=1   loop#105 started@14075 stopped=NEVER
   +3s later        : live loops = 1  (still running)
C. tab hidden       : loops=1  live=0   loop#105 stopped=@26878
```

`ambienceStop()` has exactly three call sites: inside `ambienceStart` (18430), `_vistaDismiss` (12517) and the `visibilitychange` handler (18460). **The `sndopt` handler at 25462 is not one of them** — it sets `sndOn=false` and nothing else. `ac()` gates only *new* sounds; the bed is an already-running looped `BufferSource` on the shared `_sfxBus`, and its LFO oscillator keeps running too, so the AudioContext stays alive. This is both the battery cost the reviewer asked about and, worse, audible sound after the player explicitly asked for silence.

```js
if(e.target && e.target.id==='sndopt'){
  sndOn=!sndOn;
  if(!sndOn){ try{ ambienceStop(); }catch(_){} }   // ← the bed is a LOOP; ac() only gates new sounds
  ...
}
```

Also worth deciding: nothing restarts the bed when the tab becomes visible again, so returning to a vista leaves it silent.

## CF1802-20 · The voice vocabulary is 540 entries, for an endless collection · **medium**

`voiceOf` reads exactly three things: the family (an 18-way hash of `g.seed`), `g.size % 6`, and `g.behavior % 5`. That is a closed set:

**18 × 6 × 5 = 540 possible voices. 533 distinct after clamping.**

Measured over 200,000 procedural fauna:

| collection size | chance two creatures share an identical voice |
|---|---|
| 10 | 8.6% |
| 25 | 45.0% |
| 50 | **91.3%** |
| 100 | ~100% (≈91 distinct voices among 100 creatures) |

The portrait reads fifteen-plus genes. The voice reads two genes and a hash. §2 says *"Alien creatures derive their voice from the genome, as their portrait does"* — the voice is a far lower-resolution function of the genome than the portrait, and it saturates at a collection size players reach quickly. Cheap widening: fold in `trait`, `body`, `loco` and `diet` as small multipliers on `f0`, `dur` and the filter, which costs nothing at runtime and multiplies the vocabulary by orders of magnitude without touching determinism.

## CF1802-21 · The "temperament" tilt reads a different gene, under the wrong modulus · **medium**

`voiceOf`:

```js
const bold=((+g.behavior||0)%5)/4;   /* temperament tilts the character */
```

Two problems:

1. **`behavior` is not temperament.** The card's temperament line is `FA_TEMPER[g.temper % 10]` (4506) — a separate gene. `voiceOf` never reads `g.temper`. `behavior` is the *behaviour* line ("hunting in coordinated packs", "migrating across whole continents").
2. **`FA_BEHAVIOR` has 12 entries, and the code takes `% 5`.** Distinct behaviours collapse onto the same tilt and the ordering is arbitrary:

| bold | behaviours |
|---|---|
| 0.00 | hunting in coordinated packs / grazing in vast placid herds / following seasonal blooms |
| 0.25 | migrating across continents / **fiercely territorial** / solitary and elusive |
| 0.50 | building elaborate nests / tending symbiotic flora |
| 0.75 | communicating in low infrasound / mimicking other species |
| 1.00 | **ambushing from camouflage / active only at night** |

The most aggressive string in the list carries almost the meekest voice; the two stealth behaviours carry the boldest. `bold` is a real audible parameter — it moves `f0` 33%, `rich` 47% and `dur` 21% — it is simply keyed to the wrong thing. ## CF1802-22 · `playConfirm` is defined, exported, destructured — and never called · **medium**

## CF1802-23 · The deny tone fires from a string-builder, and covers one refusal path · **medium**

```js
function _denialHTML(d){
  if(!d) return '';
  try{ playDeny(); }catch(_){ }
  return '<div class="denial">…';

```

The tone is emitted while *building markup*, not in response to a press. ## CF1802-24 · 2% of creatures voice at the 6 kHz clamp ceiling · **low**

Measured over 200,000: **3,953 (1.98%) hit the 6000 Hz ceiling — every one of them from the `bat` family** — and 1,869 (0.93%) hit the 60 Hz floor (`sessile` 1,393, `jelly` 476). At a clamp, size and temperament stop changing the voice at all, so those creatures are audibly identical to each other. 6 kHz also sits in the shrillest band of human hearing, at a fixed 0.45 gain on the reveal. Worth either lowering the `bat` `f0` (5200 is already near the ceiling before any size multiplier) or tapering gain above ~4 kHz.

Related: **5.5% of procedural creatures roll the `legacy` archetype** — the entry documented as the fallback for a genome with no rig. It is in `_VOICE_KEYS`, so it is a first-class 18th family in the wild. Probably fine, probably not intended.

- The one hard defect (CF1802-19) is a lifecycle bug, not an architecture problem.

My recommendation: fix CF1802-19, -20 and -21 first — they are hours, not weeks — then run a small human listening test. Scaling §15 against the current 540-voice vocabulary would be building on the wrong measurement.
