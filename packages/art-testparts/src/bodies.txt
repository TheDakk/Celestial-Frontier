# Every frame of a mark slot has a flow layer (column 1-15 repeating) and a spots layer (2x2 blobs).
# Four-legged torso types: shelled is the armored torso, scaffold and back parts renamed. crystalline 34x14, membranous
# 32x12, fourwinged 36x13 and spindly 28x10: rows top half 3, bottom half 2, last row a/b; head at (w-4,3), tail at (2,3),
# back at (w/2,-1), legs at row h; wing at (w-12,0) on the winged two, wing1 at (8,0) on fourwinged. Each has the seven
# back parts, drawn as the sturdy ones. Scaffolds: the armored poses, strike contact at origin x + width + 16.
# The sturdy torso is quadruped.txt's, plus a translucent layer (its outer ring at the lightest shade of its ramp).
# Wings rise from their point (near wing anchored 2 in, far wing 4 further back and 2 rows taller, so both show);
# sails rise from the back point; fins lie on the torso's side under the back point; flippers hang from near0 and near1.
# hornlet-h<n>: a small horn on the head's feature point, for every head kind but 3 and 6 (those carry a horn, so a
# tusked body asks for horns-h<n> instead).
# Every other family and shape the routed resolver produces has a torso and scaffold here (rows as above; points from
# its family's list: head (w-4,3), tail (2,3), back (w/2,-1), a serpent's eye0 (w-4,2), an insect's legs at row h).
# fish fusiform 30x12, shark 34x12, flat 32x8, angler 26x14, sturgeon 36x10; crustacean crab 30x10, lobster 34x9;
# insect beetle 28x10, generic 26x8, mantis 30x7; tentacled octopus 24x14, squid 30x10, cuttlefish 28x11; serpent
# snake 40x6, viper 36x8; gelatinous body 24x16; radial anemone 20x14, urchin 18x18, coral 22x16, cucumber 30x8.
# Heads h0-h9 (fish, crustacean, insect): (9 + n mod 4) x 8, every eye point and a feature point. Tails t2 t3 t5 t6
# (fish, crustacean, tentacled): (6 + n) x 6. eye as the four-legged eye; pit (not serpent). Insect legs: the shared
# leg, 2x6, near legs 2 columns ahead of far ones. Fish: tusks and horns for every head as the four-legged h0 ones,
# shell and crystals on the back of fusiform and sturgeon. Back parts are left out: a missing one is information.
# Non-medium tiers draw the family fallback.
# Crustacean fallbacks: a body a quarter of the canvas each way, two claws above its ends.
