### ★★ THE RULE THIS DOCUMENT KEPT TEACHING, STATED ONCE
Three separate fixes in this file were **right about the defect and wrong about the remedy**,
all in the same direction, all caught by artlock:
- **G11, the primate torso wash** — a fixed cream over an arbitrary hue *is* a stain. Deleting
  it: 1007 → 1018, Aye-Aye in seven of eleven new pairs.
- **G6, the dark birds' bills** — a crow's bill *is* black. Making it so: 1003 → 1016, Raven
  in six of fifteen.
- **G11's snail rim, inverted** — the one that worked, because it *replaced* the stroke rather
  than removing it.

> ★ **ON A DARK ANIMAL, THE ONLY LIGHT ELEMENT IS STRUCTURAL.** Removing it for accuracy costs
> more legibility than the accuracy buys — unless you put the contrast back somewhere else.
> A subtraction that improves fidelity and deletes the last tonal separation is a regression,
> and the fingerprint will say so before a human notices.

### ❌ G11's ape wash — DO NOT SIMPLY DELETE IT
The prescription is *"delete the pale blotch pass on primate torsos"*, and the diagnosis is
right: a **fixed** cream over an arbitrary hue is a stain, not shading. It is also
**load-bearing**. Deleting it took confusable **1007 → 1018**, with the **Aye-Aye alone in
seven of the eleven new pairs** — stripped of the wash a dark primate is a uniform silhouette
and matches every other small dark subject. Re-deriving it from `p` (×1.52) gave 1016,
because on a near-black ape a proportional lift is no lift at all.
**The wash is the only tonal modelling on that torso — that is the actual defect.** Removing
it requires real countershading off the body's own form (`countershade()`, which the mammals
got in wave 4) to replace it. A subtraction alone is a regression.

### ❌ G9 — WHAT I TRIED, WHY IT FAILED, AND WHAT IT ACTUALLY NEEDS
The diagnosis was right and the fix was not. **The reported "hard STRAIGHT VERTICAL clipped
right edge" is `closePath()`** — the fan's path ends at (fx+0.40·fw, fy+0.18·fh) and closes
with a straight chord back up to its start at (fx+0.42·fw, fy−0.46·fh). It was never a clip.

Three attempts, all reverted, all worse than what they replaced:
1. **Draw the fan before the head** (which its own comment has falsely claimed since arc
   stage 3). Correct in principle — but the fan's outline was *authored to sit on top of*
   the head, so with the skull over it the visible remainder is a wedge.
2. **Move it forward** (−1.22 → −0.86, then −1.12) so the skull covers only its front
   margin. Both put the fan on the FACE; the head then cuts a hard diagonal out of it and it
   reads as a paper wedge stuck to the head.
3. **Replace the closing chord with a bezier.** Bulging it forward (0.60·fw) to hide the
   straight edge put a lobe over the eye.

**What it actually needs:** the fan re-authored as a shape that assumes the head is in front
of it — root narrow and tucked behind the jaw hinge, widening down and back, with the top
fold as a separate overlapping piece. That is a rewrite of the branch, not a nudge to its
anchor. **Do not move `fx` without redrawing the outline.**
⚠ Note also that wave 36 moved `fx` −1.02 → −1.22 to keep the fan off the cheek. That was
treating a symptom of the draw order, and it is what put the ear on the shoulder.

### G1 — The round ear renders as a spiral horn disc on the crown ★ biggest single win

**Species reported (FAIL):** Jaguar, Leopard, Tiger, Clouded Leopard, Ocelot, Cougar, Panda,
Red Panda, Possum, Sloth Bear, Spectacled Bear, Kinkajou, Coati, Pangolin, Sloth, Meerkat.
**(POLISH):** Stoat, and every other `earShape:'round'` row.
**Blast radius:** the default ear for families `felid`, `mustelid`, `rodent`, `marsupial`,
`hyaenid`, `generic` — roughly **60+ of the 144 mammals** on the quadruped system.

**Shared cause.** `packages/art/src/quadrupedoverrides.ts`, ear block ≈ L1160–1265:

```
const ex = headX - headR * (s < 0 ? 0.62 : 0.24);        // pair only 0.38·headR apart
const ey = headY - headR * 0.42 - earR * 0.52;           // both ABOVE the crown
```

For `ears:'round'`, `earR = 0.38·headR` and the cup is `0.92·earR` wide, so the near and far
ear **overlap into one mass sitting on top of the skull**. Each is filled coat×0.86 (near) /
×0.62 (far), then a concha at coat×0.46. Result: **two nested two-tone ellipses on the crown
= concentric rings = a coiled horn or a shell.** Nine separate verify notes independently
describe it as "a spiral horn-disc", "a coiled horn/shell", "a concentric spiral disc where
the ear should be". They are all looking at the same twelve lines.

**Fix.**
1. Root the pair on the **side** of the skull, not above it — `ey` at or below `headY − 0.2·headR`.
2. Separate the roots to **≥ 2·earR** so near and far never overlap.
3. Replace the concentric concha ellipse with a soft-edged crescent open toward the front.
4. **Verify at 128px.** This defect is invisible at master size and unmistakable on a tile.

*Second-order effect:* fixing G1 alone turns "reads as a goat/ram" into "reads as a cat" for
the entire felid block, because the crown horn is doing more damage than the coat pattern.

### G2 — `horn:'curl'` draws a closed ring across the face

**Species (FAIL):** Gaur, Banteng, Water Buffalo, Buffalo — all four reported independently as
"wearing a cream donut". **(POLISH):** Sheep, Takin, Mountain Goat.
**Also on this token, unaudited:** Ibex, Tahr, Goat, Musk Ox, Yak, Wildebeest. **~13 species.**

**Shared cause.** `quadrupedoverrides.ts` L1386–1391:

```
c.arc(headX - headR*0.1 + s*headR*0.5, headY - headR*0.2, headR*0.66, -0.4, 4.2, s < 0);
```

`-0.4 → 4.2` is **4.6 rad ≈ 264°** at `lineWidth 9` with round caps. The tips come within a
stroke width of each other, so it closes into a **torus**, and its centre is on the *face*
(`headY − 0.2·headR`), not above it. Impossible geometry; reads as a pasted ring at any size.
Takin's verify note found the same ring drawn straight across the eye line.

**Fix.**
1. Cap any horn arc at **≤ 200°** and assert `|end − start| · r > 3 · lineWidth` between tips.
2. Origin at the **poll** — above and behind the eye, never over the muzzle.
3. Split the token: bovines get two open crescents sweeping **out then up/back**; caprids get
   a backward curl over the neck; Buffalo additionally needs the fused forehead **boss**.

### G3 — 22 snakes on one painter, separated only by hue

**Species (FAIL):** Anaconda, Boa, Python, Cobra, Viper. **(POLISH):** Rat Snake, Grass Snake,
Sand Boa, Tree Snake, Garter Snake. **All 22 `reptSnake` call sites** in
`packages/art/src/faunaoverrides2.ts` (painter at L82) differ *only* by `hue` plus three
booleans `{hood, rattle, banded}`.

**Four shared defects, in fix order:**

1. **The head is a pasted oval with a hard bright rim.** Reported verbatim on Anaconda, Boa,
   Python, Rat Snake, Grass Snake, Sand Boa. It is composited as a separate ellipse with no
   neck continuity. → Grow the head out of the neck at equal width and taper it forward; kill
   the rim stroke entirely.
2. **No dorsal pattern axis exists.** Python has no reticulation, Boa no saddles, Anaconda no
   oval blotches, Viper no zigzag chain, Garter Snake no longitudinal stripes. Hue cannot
   separate 22 snakes and the pattern is the identity in every one of these species.
   → Add `pattern: 'reticulate' | 'saddle' | 'oval' | 'zigzag' | 'stripe' | 'plain'`.
3. **The coil closes into a torus with no tail tip** (Anaconda, Boa, Sand Boa, Rat Snake).
   Python is the exception — it *does* show a tip, so the geometry can do it.
   → Always terminate in a visible taper that crosses over the body.
4. **The transverse ribbing reads as earthworm annulation** (Viper, Cobra, Garter Snake all
   independently named "worm"). → Overlapping scale rows, not ring shading.

**Cobra additionally:** `hood:true` must imply a **reared posture** (the row specifies aspect
1.1, taller than wide; the render is 2:1 wide) with the hood as flat skin *behind* the head.
Currently it is a frontal ribbed shield pasted onto a side-view body — a perspective mismatch,
which is why it reads as armour.

### G5 — The pasted-ellipse wing and breast patch

**Species (FAIL):** Eagle — the worst artifact in the whole pass: a straight wing blade
crossing the body with no shoulder join, plus **five separate feather lozenges floating clear

### G9 — The elephant/hippo ear is a ribbed plate bolted to the shoulder

**Species (FAIL):** Asian Elephant. **(POLISH):** Elephant, Forest Elephant, African Elephant,
Hippopotamus. **5 species.**

**Shared cause.** `quadrupedoverrides.ts`, the `ears === 'fan'` branch: `fx = headX −
1.22·headR` sets the fan so far back that it lands on the **shoulder, behind the head**, and
the four straight parallel vein strokes read as a grille/radiator. Asian Elephant's verify

real clipping artifact, not just a placement error. The code's own comment claims "drawn
behind the head so the head overlaps its root"; it is drawn *after* the head, so it never was.
That false claim is now on its third recorded generation (D-ART-137, wave 36, this pass).

**Fix.** Draw the fan **before** the head. Bring `fx` forward so the root sits on the skull
behind the eye. Soften the outline, curve the veins and cut their count. Asian and Forest need
a genuinely smaller, more triangular fan — it is one of only two traits separating them from
African, and `earScale` has a documented history of not reaching this branch.

## 4. POLISH (590) — by theme, not by row

Nothing in POLISH is an identity failure. A stranger names the animal correctly; a named trait
is missing or a shape is pasted. Approximate weights from the 143 rows I hold:

1. **A named colour trait is simply not painted (~35%).** The largest single bucket and the
   cheapest to clear — these are table edits, not painter work. White rump patch (Banteng),
   black stockings (Fox, Red Fox, Maned Wolf), white eye ring, black cap (Tern), red legs and
   bill (Chough), chest blaze, pollen baskets (Bee, Honeybee), black abdomen banding
   (Honeybee — "not one black band"), white neck ring (Pheasant), pale spots on dark ground
   (Char — currently **inverted**), pale back stripes (Snipe), yellow collar (Grass Snake).
   *Many of these are one hex value in the species row.*

2. **Wrong token borrowed from the wrong family (~20%).** Tail, ear or bill token picked from
   a sibling: Parrot on the macaw tail, Ground Squirrel on the tree-squirrel plume, Marten and
   Mongoose on a stub where the row wants a full-length tail, Goose/Duck/Swan on a pointed
   cone bill, Pigeon on a mallard head cap. Usually a one-word change plus, occasionally, a
   new token.

3. **Pasted-shape seams (~15%).** The G5/G8/G9/G11 signature showing up in POLISH severity:
   wing ellipses, breast patches, hump domes, coat panels, tail-root discs (Wild Pony —
   "a flat pale ellipse laid over the rump with bristles emerging from behind it"), armour
   rectangles (Armadillo), spiny dorsal bands whose straight base does not follow the curved
   back (Tilapia). **Fixing G5 and G8 clears most of this bucket for free.**

4. **A small diagnostic is missing (~15%).** Barbels (Carp), adipose fin (Salmon, Trout),
   tympanum (Bullfrog — the row's *first* mustRead), brow shovel (Reindeer, Caribou), cheek
   flanges (Orangutan), casque (Guineafowl), suckers (Leech), proboscis (Mosquito — and four
   wings drawn where a mosquito has two), toe discs (Tree Frog), dewlap and shoulder hump
   (Eland). Each is a small addition to one painter.

5. **Proportion misses (~10%).** Eel at 5:1 against a spec of 12:1; Jackrabbit's hind legs as
   small nubs; Spoonbill with no neck at all; Kingfisher given a visible neck when *no visible
   neck* is the diagnostic; Hyena drawn level when the shoulder-to-hip slope is the animal.

6. **Near-duplicate pairs at POLISH severity (~5%).** See G10.

## 5. What this pass proves — defects now confirmed FIXED

Real progress, stated as precisely as the failures. Each of these was flagged by the old
audits and is **absent** from this pass, or was actively refuted on re-check:

- **D-ART-137 — `earShape:'hidden'` used `return` and skipped the whole face.** Sloth, Mole,
  Seal, Fur Seal, Sea Lion and Walrus rendered with a blank head and no eye; Walrus lost its
  tusks. **FIXED.** Sloth now fails on posture and claws; Walrus fails only on a missing
  bristle moustache; none of the six shows a missing face. Zero rows in this pass report a
  blank head.
- **D-ART-136 / wave 36 — the ear pair merging into "a single giant disc-ear pasted over the
  skull"** on Fennec Fox, Serval, Sand Cat, Maned Wolf, African Wild Dog, Polar Bear, Wild Ass.
  **FIXED for all seven** — none appears as FAIL. Maned Wolf and Pampas Fox survive at POLISH
  for a *different* reason (a faceted origami wedge). The merge is gone; the shape is now wrong.
- **D-ART-133 — the felid paw drawn *inside* the limb, "15 of 17 felid rows reported hoof-like
  tips, no paws".** **PARTLY FIXED — and this matters.** Leopard's re-check explicitly corrected
  the first report from "dark cloven hooves" to "pale rounded stubs": the paw *is* being
  painted. It is now a **legibility** problem at tile scale (drawn at coat×0.52, too low in
  contrast and too small to resolve), not an absent-geometry problem. Do not re-plumb the foot
  switch; raise the paw's contrast and size.
- **Stroke bleed is largely solved.** Three of five claims refuted at 7×+ (Tiger, Okapi,
  Anaconda). Clipping holds on the big coat passes. Five genuine cases remain (G8 box).
- **Anaconda body mass** — the old audit had "not massively thick" failing; the re-check
  confirms **mustRead 1 is now met**, and there is a real inner coil rather than a closed torus.
- **Python tail tip** — now visible (a hooked taper inside the inner coil). The "no tail tip"
  claim was refuted for Python specifically, which proves the coil geometry can terminate.
- **Mantis raptorial foreleg** — a Z-folded foreleg **is** now drawn. It is bare and thin, but
  "no raptorial forelegs" is refuted.
- **Mountain Tapir proboscis** — present, enlarged, overhanging and drooping past the lower
  jaw. The old "no proboscis, plain blunt snout" is **wrong**; and its head is *not* the
  Sloth Bear head. It fails on the shared body, not the nose.
- **Gorilla brow ridge** — a heavy dark brow bar is present. "No brow ridge" refuted; the
  genuine gaps are the sagittal crest and the knuckle-walking hands.
- **Land Snail head and eyes** — at 8× there **are** two dark eye knobs on the upper tentacle
  pair and a soft head mass at their base. "No head, no eyes" refuted; it fails on a stray
  stroke, a slab foot and a detached shadow.
- **Cobra hood** — a hood mass is present. "No hood" refuted; it fails on perspective and on
  not being reared.
- **Tortoise shell scutes** — the radial+concentric line work over a filled dome is a fair
  schematic of scute division, not the "hollow wireframe" first reported. Fails on the legs
  and the missing beak instead.
- **Takin body** — called *"the best in this set"*: stocky, humped shoulders, short thick legs,
  long shaggy golden coat, Roman-nosed muzzle. The body system works when the parameters are
  right. Its FAIL is one horn arc (G2) and two coat panels (G8).

**The meta-lesson, recorded three times now in the source comments and once more here:**
`quadrupedoverrides.ts` L1195 (*"WAVE 13 SHIPPED HALF A FIX… a fix that covers part of its own
surface reads as done and is not"*), D-ART-137 (*"a fix can be correct about the thing it names
and wrong about where it stops"*), and the elephant-ear comment that has claimed "drawn behind
the head" through three generations while never being true. **Every fix in section 2 must be
verified by rendering the full affected species list at tile size, not by reading the table
and not by rendering one exemplar.**
