# 1. Executive verdict

This is a **genuine and substantial improvement** over the previous submission.

The new output resolves several major integrity and morphology problems:

- the two Earth archives contain the same 1,010 assets, only under different root-folder names;
- the earlier 1,014-versus-1,010 count conflict is resolved;
- Reindeer Lichen now belongs only to fungi;
- Snow Algae now belongs only to microbes;
- Green Algae now belongs only to flora;
- Tardigrade now belongs only to fauna;
- no byte-for-byte duplicate portraits remain anywhere in the unique 1,250-asset output;
- many previously incorrect specialist organisms now have recognizable body/growth forms;
- procedural fungi and microbes now show meaningful structural variation rather than one recolored template.

However:

> **The current Earth catalog is not yet Platinum for strict real-world accuracy.**

The principal remaining issue is still the underlying shared scaffold. Many organisms now have better markings or signature parts, but the torso, head, limbs, rear, or growth habit still belong to a different generalized family.

This review uses three statuses:

- **PASS:** leave alone at the current stylized resolution.
- **HOLD:** correctly routed and broadly recognizable, but not yet species-specific enough for Platinum approval.
- **FAIL:** clear anatomical, growth-form, or morphology correction is required.

# 4. Remaining global-body-template failures

## Equids

Horse remains the clearest example, and the problem affects:

- Horse;
- Wild Horse;
- Donkey;
- Wild Ass;
- Wild Pony;
- Zebra.

They need a dedicated equid scaffold with:

- deep barrel torso;
- withers;
- sloped shoulders;
- muscular hindquarters;
- long wedge-shaped head;
- articulated knees/hocks/fetlocks/pasterns;
- real hooves;
- mane;
- correctly attached tail.

## Felids

The markings are often good, but many cats still inherit:

- an ungulate-like torso;
- straight stilt legs;
- hoof-like feet;
- weak feline shoulders;
- insufficiently feline skulls.

The whole felid family needs a true feline base before applying stripes, spots, manes, and color.

## Canids

Many dogs, wolves, foxes, coyotes, jackals, and related animals remain deer-like.

They need:

- canine chest and waist;
- digitigrade legs and paws;
- canine skull/muzzle;
- correct ears;
- a full brush tail;
- species-specific body mass.

## Bears and camels

Several bears remain pig/sheep/ungulate-like.

Camels continue to look like humps added to deer bodies.

These need dedicated bear and camelid scaffolds.

## Crocodilians

Alligator, Caiman, Crocodile, and Gharial remain too narrow and lizard-like.

They need:

- wide low torsos;
- robust splayed limbs;
- species-correct skulls;
- armored backs;
- thick muscular tails.

## Specialist birds

Raptors, seabirds, waders, gamebirds, parrots, and flightless birds still share too much of one compact side-bird scaffold.

## Insects

Many insects still need:

- clearer head/thorax/abdomen segmentation;
- six articulated legs;
- correct wing count and attachment;
- antennae and mouthparts;
- species-specific shell/body proportions.

## Specialist fish and marine animals

The library still overuses generic torpedo, eel, shell, whale, and jelly body types.
