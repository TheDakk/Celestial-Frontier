## 2 · Five things nine rounds add that v4.0 does not carry

### 2.1 A second RNG domain, so outcomes are replayable

§16.2 specifies world determinism thoroughly and Gate B requires "no uncontrolled clock/randomness" in domain packages. Both are about the *universe*. Neither makes a **player outcome** reproducible.

Measured in v1.8.6 — 22 `Math.random()` call sites, all outside the domain modules, so this is consistent with §3.8's domain-module exclusion pass rather than contradicting it:

| purpose | sites | functions |
|---|---:|---|
| cosmetic — particle scatter, noise buffers | 11 | `fxBurst`, `playVoice`, `playHit`, `playWhoosh`, `ambienceStart` |
| **outcome rolls** | **11** | `tryCapture`, `openPicker`, `_descRoll`, `attemptContact`, `hazardFlavor`, `_tutGrant`, `_tutDuel` |

The universe is reproducible; what happens to the player is not. That is correct design — nobody should be able to seed-scum a capture — but it has two costs the port can remove for free:

- **No test can assert an outcome.** A capture, a descent roll, a hazard, a training grant: none can be pinned in a fixture.
- **No bug report can be replayed.** Across rounds 7–9 several probes had to be re-run because `_tutGrant` rolls unseeded and the training cache changed underneath them.

**Proposal:** two named domains, both typed, neither optional.

- **`WorldRNG`** — seeded, pure, reproducible. Everything in generation. Already effectively true; §16.2 formalises it.
- **`SessionRNG`** — seeded once per session from a stored value the player never sees, advanced deterministically thereafter. Outcomes stay unpredictable *to the player* and become perfectly replayable *to a test or a bug report*, because the session seed goes in the save and in the diagnostics export.

This belongs in §16.2 as a second subsection, and in Gate B as "outcome rolls draw from `SessionRNG`; a recorded session replays identically."

*Worth confirming against v1.8.9 — my count is from v1.8.6 and the app sections may have moved.*

### 2.2 Reachability has units, and the number is the headline

§4.6, §4.7 and Risk 12 establish the principle. What they do not carry is the measurement, and the measurement is more alarming than the principle suggests.

The round-8/9 fleet records three counters per player verb:

- **`did`** — the verb completed and the world changed
- **`saw`** — the affordance existed when we looked for it
- **`goalFail`** — it existed and pressing it changed nothing

**Once an affordance is on screen, the game works.** Deep-tier reach in v1.8.6: `mine`, `harvest`, `scavenge`, `tame`, `conquer`, `feed`, `charter`, `idle`, `backout` all **100%**; `breed` 96%; `craft` 94%. Nothing in the mid-game is broken.

**Almost nothing gets on screen.** The dominant failure is the affordance not existing when the player looks:

| verb | found no card | succeeded |
|---|---:|---:|
| harvest | **109** | 9 |
| tame | **89** | 2 |
| scavenge | **84** | 3 |
| conquer | **45** | 3 |

Those verbs only exist on a world you are standing on, so the chain is *find a landable world → land → dismiss a vista → then look*, and it fails roughly nine times in ten.

The same shape in the economy: **52 of 62 recipes need intermediate parts**, 16 need a prerequisite item, 9 need a Prime Codex signature — about ten are makeable from raw materials — and every Fabricator fold starts closed, so a full cargo hold shows almost nothing and never shows the chain.

**This is a design finding, not an architecture one, which is why §6 does not have a home for it.** It belongs in the port because Phase 4 rebuilds exactly these surfaces, and the fix is cheap while they are being rebuilt and expensive afterwards: a "makeable now" filter with a two-step chain hint (`_craftNeed` already computes the shortfall for the button face), folds that open when they contain something actionable, and one mid-game goal surfaced early.

### 2.3 Engaging with the systems currently costs you on both counters

From the round-8 archetype table, deep sessions, which I have not seen stated anywhere:

| | Δ codex | Δ stardust |
|---|---:|---:|
| breeder | **−21** | 78 |
| min-maxer | −17 | 40 |
| collector | −10 | 36 |
| miner | 0 | **28** |
| — | | |
| button-masher | +1 | **108** |
| explorer | +1 | 85 |
| cautious-newbie | 0 | 82 |

Every union consumes both parents and every meal consumes a flora, so the archetypes engaging most deeply watch their collection shrink; and the archetypes that spend on the economy end poorer than the ones that ignore it. Both are arithmetically correct and neither is framed on screen as progress.

Not a bug, and not necessarily wrong — but it is a balance decision the port should make deliberately rather than inherit. It belongs in §23's open-items table.

### 2.4 The persona fleet as a named gate

§5 credits the existing test culture including synthetic players, and Risk 12 names the failure mode. The gates in §22 do not include a reach metric.

Proposal for **Gate H (feature-complete beta)**: 18 archetypes × 12 goal-directed verbs, with `saw`/`did`/`goalFail` recorded, and two thresholds — **`did`/`saw` ≥ 95% per verb** (the system works when found) and **`saw`/attempt not materially worse than the v1.8.9 baseline** (the system is no harder to find than it is today). The second is what stops the port quietly losing reachability during the component rewrite; the first is what stops it shipping a system that is present and inert.

The harness is written and runs against the current build. It is in the round-8 bundle with a README.

### 2.5 The audio vocabulary number that justifies §15's shape

§15.1's recommendation — preserve the identity model, rebuild the architecture — is right, and here is the measurement that supports it. 200,000 procedural fauna through the build's own `voiceOf`:

| | v1.8.2 | v1.8.6 |
|---|---:|---:|
| distinct voices | 533 of 20,000 — a closed set of **540** | **199,707 of 200,000** |
| creatures sharing a voice with another | 97.3% | **0.15%** |
| duplicate inside a 50-creature collection | **91.3%** | **0.6%** |
| pinned at the 6 kHz clamp ceiling | 1.98% | 0.83% |

The identity model was worth preserving only *after* v1.8.5–1.8.6 widened it; at 540 voices a player exhausted the vocabulary at about fifty creatures. It is now effectively unique-per-creature, which is what makes "port `voiceOf(g)` and preserve identity through golden fixtures" the correct call rather than a sentimental one.

It also means **the human listening test in §15.1 is now worth running** — the precondition is met, and it should happen before any of §15.20's production commitment.

## 3 · Two corrections to my own prior reports

**Dual rarity.** §3.6 is new to me and corrects a conflation running through rounds 7–9. I have been writing "tier 12–14", "tier ≥ 8 Mythic", "TIER_MAX" as though there were one ladder. There are two — a 10-name display ladder and a 15-band internal grade ladder — and several of my exploit payoff estimates quoted raw-band names as though players see them. The findings stand; the *labels* in them should be read as `RawGradeTier`. §16.3's `RawGradeTier` / `DisplayRarityTier` split is the right fix and I will use those terms from here.

**`COSMIC_EPOCH` beats my proposal.** For CF1805-05 and CF1806-03 I recommended a load-time bound derived from the save's own `at` stamp, on the reasoning that an in-memory monotonic stamp cannot bind an attacker who controls the reload. v1.8.9's answer — play-time regeneration on a persisted `COSMIC_EPOCH` — is strictly better: it is monotonic, it survives the reload, and it does not depend on trusting any wall clock at load. That generalises to every cooldown in the game and it should be the port's single time authority.
